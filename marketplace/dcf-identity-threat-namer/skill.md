# Dcf Identity Threat Namer

Name the identity threat under the conversation for both of you. Hard conversations feel hard because they threaten how we see ourselves: as competent, as good, as worthy. Ground yourself before the conversation so a single comment does not knock you off balance.

## Inputs

- `identity-conversation`: What the situation says to each of you about who you are
- `feelings-inventory`: The feelings you plan to name, which often trace back to identity

## Outputs

- `identity-threats`: The specific self-image each person is defending, for you and for them
- `grounding-reframes`: Reframes that let you hold the threat without collapsing or counterattacking

## Process

1. Name your own identity threat in one sentence: the thing you are afraid this conversation proves about you.
2. Replace the all-or-nothing version with a complex one. 'I am either a good manager or a failure' becomes 'I am a capable manager who handled this part poorly.' Black-and-white identities shatter under pressure.
3. Guess their identity threat and plan to avoid stepping on it. If they fear looking incompetent, lead with contribution and curiosity, not with a verdict.
4. Write one grounding line to say to yourself if you feel the threat spike mid-conversation, so you can stay present instead of defending.
