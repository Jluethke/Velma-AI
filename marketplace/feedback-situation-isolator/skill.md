# Feedback Situation Isolator

Isolate the specific situation where the behavior occurred. Feedback without a specific situation becomes a character judgment. 'In last Tuesday's client call' not 'generally speaking.'

## Inputs

- `behavior-to-address`: What behavior you need to give feedback on
- `recipient-context`: Your relationship to the person and the context in which you work together

## Outputs

- `situation-anchor`: The specific date, time, meeting, conversation, or event where the behavior occurred
- `pattern-or-one-off`: Whether this is a single instance or a pattern, and the evidence for each

## Process

1. Name the exact situation: date, event, participants. 'In the Tuesday 3pm product review with the engineering team.'
2. Determine if this is a pattern or a one-off. A pattern requires at least 3 instances. Two is a coincidence, not a pattern.
3. If it is a pattern, list the instances. If it is a one-off, acknowledge that in your delivery. 'I noticed something in Tuesday's meeting' lands differently than 'You always do this.'
4. Feedback on a pattern is harder to deliver but more important. Feedback on a one-off should be lighter in weight.
