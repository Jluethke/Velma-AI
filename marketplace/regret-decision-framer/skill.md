# Regret Decision Framer

Frame the decision as a choice between action and inaction. Most life decisions can be reduced to: do I try this or not? The framing matters because regret research shows a clear asymmetry: people regret things they did not do far more than things they did and failed at.

## Inputs

- `decision-description`: The life or career decision you are facing
- `current-situation`: Where you are now and what changes if you do nothing

## Outputs

- `action-option`: The specific action: what you would do, by when, at what cost
- `inaction-option`: What your life looks like in 1, 5, and 10 years if you do not take this action

## Process

1. State the action option in specific terms: what exactly would you do, by when, and what does it cost you?
2. State the inaction option with equal specificity: what does your life look like in 1, 5, and 10 years if you stay on the current path?
3. Do not make the inaction option sound worse than it is. Be honest about the benefits of stability.
4. Do not make the action option sound better than it is. Be honest about the risks.
