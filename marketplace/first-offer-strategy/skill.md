# First Offer Strategy

Decide whether to make the first offer and what it should be, using anchoring theory.

## Inputs

- `reservation-price`: Your floor
- `counterparty-reservation-estimate`: Their estimated floor

## Outputs

- `first-offer-decision`: Whether to make the first offer (yes if you have good information about the ZOPA, no if you are uncertain about their valuation)
- `anchor-offer`: If making the first offer: the specific number, set aggressively but justifiably within the ZOPA

## Process

1. Make the first offer when you have good information about the ZOPA. The first offer anchors the negotiation: all subsequent offers are adjustments from this anchor.
2. Set the anchor aggressively but justifiably. If the ZOPA is $85K-$100K and you are selling, anchor at $110K with a justification (comparable deals, market rate, value delivered). The counterparty will negotiate down, but from your anchor.
3. Do not make the first offer when you have poor information. If you do not know the counterparty's BATNA, let them anchor first; their offer reveals information about their valuation.
4. Always justify the first offer. An anchor without justification feels arbitrary and provokes a negative reaction. An anchor with justification ('Based on comparable deals and the value delivered, the right number is $110K') feels like a data point.
