# Audience Belief Inventory

Map what the audience already believes about the topic before you say anything. List their assumptions, prior experiences, and emotional associations. If you skip this step, you are guessing at a frame instead of building on one.

## Inputs

- `topic-or-proposal`: The thing you need to communicate
- `audience-description`: Who you are communicating to (role, relationship, context)

## Outputs

- `belief-map`: List of the audience's existing beliefs, assumptions, and emotional associations about this topic
- `knowledge-gaps`: What the audience does not know or has wrong that matters for your message

## Process

1. Write down everything the audience likely believes about this topic based on their role, past interactions, and context.
2. For each belief, note whether it helps your message (an anchor you can build on) or hurts it (a frame you need to reframe).
3. Identify knowledge gaps: things they do not know that would change their position if they did.
4. Flag emotional associations. A CFO hearing 'new platform' feels risk. A designer hearing 'standardize' feels constraint. Name the feeling, not just the fact.
