# Case Study Documentation

Convert the interview transcripts into case studies. Each case study preserves the context, the judgment, and the reasoning. Case studies are more valuable than procedures because they preserve the 'why.'

## Inputs

- `incident-transcripts`: Interview transcripts from critical incident interviews
- `judgment-pattern-notes`: Notes on recurring patterns and heuristics

## Outputs

- `case-study-library`: Library of case studies organized by domain, each following the situation-cues-judgment-outcome-lesson format
- `heuristic-catalog`: Catalog of expert heuristics (rules of thumb) extracted from the case studies, each with the context where it applies and where it breaks down

## Process

1. Format each incident as a case study with five sections: Situation (what was happening), Cues (what the expert noticed), Judgment (what they decided and why), Outcome (what happened), and Lesson (what a less experienced person should learn from this).
2. From the collection of case studies, extract the heuristics: the rules of thumb that experts apply repeatedly. For each heuristic, document when it applies, when it breaks down, and what exception would override it.
3. Have the knowledge holder review each case study for accuracy. Their correction is itself valuable data, so note what they change and why.
4. Store the case studies alongside the related procedures. When someone reads the procedure for 'how to do X,' the linked case studies show 'what to do when X goes wrong in non-obvious ways.'
