# Light Exposure Protocol

Design your light exposure: bright light within 30 minutes of waking, dim light 2 hours before sleep, zero blue light in the last hour.

## Inputs

- `sleep-baseline`: Your current sleep data
- `sleep-patterns`: Identified patterns

## Outputs

- `light-protocol`: Morning: 10+ minutes of bright outdoor light within 30 minutes of waking. Evening: dim warm light after sunset. Last hour: no screens or blue-blocking glasses.
- `environment-changes`: Physical changes to implement: blackout curtains, warm-spectrum evening bulbs, morning light alarm

## Process

1. Morning light exposure is the most important circadian signal. Get outside within 30 minutes of waking for 10+ minutes. Overcast days still provide 10x more lux than indoor lighting.
2. Evening light exposure is the most common sleep disruptor. After sunset, switch to warm-spectrum (below 3000K) bulbs. Standard LED bulbs emit blue wavelengths that suppress melatonin production.
3. In the last hour before bed, eliminate screens or use blue-blocking glasses. The evidence for blue-blocking glasses is moderate; the evidence for eliminating screens entirely is strong.
4. Blackout curtains are non-negotiable if your bedroom gets any outside light. Even small amounts of light through the eyelids disrupt deep sleep stages.
