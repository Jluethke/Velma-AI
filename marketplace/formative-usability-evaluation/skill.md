# Formative Usability Evaluation

Conduct formative usability studies during design to identify and fix usability problems before the design is finalized.

## Inputs

- `use-related-risk-analysis`: Identified use errors and risk controls to evaluate
- `device-user-interface`: Current prototype or mockup of the user interface

## Outputs

- `formative-findings`: Usability problems identified, root causes, severity, and design recommendations
- `design-iterations`: Interface modifications made in response to formative findings

## Process

1. Plan formative studies early and iterate. Five to eight representative users per round is sufficient to identify major usability problems (Nielsen's research shows 5 users find ~85% of problems). Run multiple rounds as the design evolves.
2. Use think-aloud protocol: ask participants to verbalize their thoughts while performing tasks with the prototype. Observe without coaching. Record task completion success, errors, hesitations, and participant comments. Difficulty is data.
3. After each round, categorize findings by root cause: is this a perception problem (display too dim, label too small), cognition problem (ambiguous terminology, non-intuitive workflow), or action problem (control too close to another, force too high)? The root cause determines the fix.
4. For critical tasks (identified from risk analysis), track whether the design change actually prevented the use error. If a user still makes the error after the redesign, the fix did not work. Iterate until the critical-task error rate reaches an acceptable level.
5. Document formative findings and design iterations in the usability engineering file. Formative studies are not pass/fail. Their purpose is to improve the design. The summative validation study provides the pass/fail evidence.
