# Knowledge Inventory

Inventory the knowledge to be transferred: explicit knowledge (documented procedures) and tacit knowledge (judgment, exceptions, patterns).

## Inputs

- `knowledge-holder`: The person or team transferring knowledge (their role, tenure, and area of expertise)
- `knowledge-receiver`: The person or team receiving knowledge (their current skill level and learning style)

## Outputs

- `explicit-knowledge-list`: Documented procedures, guides, and references that already exist
- `tacit-knowledge-list`: The judgment calls, exceptions, patterns, and undocumented expertise that exist only in the knowledge holder's head

## Process

1. List the explicit knowledge: what is documented? Where is it? Is it current? Explicit knowledge is the easy part: hand over the documents.
2. Interview the knowledge holder to surface tacit knowledge. Ask: 'What do you know that is not written down anywhere?' 'What are the exceptions to the documented procedures?' 'When do you override the standard process, and why?' 'What are the three things that would take a new person the longest to figure out?'
3. Categorize the tacit knowledge: decision-making judgment (when to deviate from the standard), relationship knowledge (who to call for what), pattern recognition (what signals indicate a problem before it manifests), and workarounds (undocumented solutions to recurring issues).
4. Prioritize by criticality: which tacit knowledge, if lost, would cause the most damage? Transfer that first.
