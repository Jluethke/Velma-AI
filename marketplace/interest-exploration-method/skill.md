# Interest Exploration Method

Explore interests beneath positions. When the other person states a position, ask why it matters to them. Beneath every position is an interest that can be met in multiple ways.

## Inputs

- `your-interests`: Your underlying interests
- `their-likely-interests`: Your estimate of their interests, to be updated during the conversation

## Outputs

- `shared-interests`: Interests that both parties share (these are the foundation for agreement)
- `different-but-compatible-interests`: Interests that differ but do not conflict (these allow creative trades)
- `conflicting-interests`: Interests that genuinely conflict (these require compromise or objective criteria)

## Process

1. When they state a position, ask 'Why is that important to you?' or 'What would that give you?' to uncover the interest beneath the position.
2. When you state your interest, explain why: 'I need [interest] because [reason].' The reason helps the other person understand what would satisfy you, not just what you are demanding.
3. Sort the interests into three categories: shared (you both want the project to succeed), different but compatible (you want credit and they want learning: both achievable), and conflicting (you both want the same limited resource).
4. For shared interests, acknowledge them explicitly: 'We both want [shared interest]. That is our common ground.' For conflicting interests, do not pretend they do not exist. Name them and address them next.
