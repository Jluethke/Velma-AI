# Outcome Based Jd Writer

Write the job description based on outcomes, not responsibilities. The job description leads with the outcomes the person will produce, not the tasks they will perform. This attracts candidates who think in terms of results, not activities. It also makes performance evaluation straightforward: did you produce the outcomes?

## Inputs

- `coherence-assessment`: Whether the role is coherent or needs splitting
- `role-structure-recommendation`: How the role is structured

## Outputs

- `outcome-based-job-description`: The job description structured around outcomes (what you will accomplish) rather than responsibilities (what you will do)
- `evaluation-criteria`: How performance will be measured, derived directly from the outcomes

## Process

1. Open the job description with the outcomes: 'In your first year, you will...' followed by each outcome with its metric and target.
2. Below the outcomes, list the activities as context (not as the primary content): 'To produce these outcomes, you will...'
3. Replace generic requirements ('strong analytical skills') with the specific skills required by the activities ('able to analyze a 10,000-row dataset to identify the top 3 bottlenecks in a support workflow').
4. Include the evaluation criteria: 'Your success in this role will be measured by...' followed by the outcomes. This signals to candidates that results matter, not just effort.
