# Conversation Preparation

Prepare before the conversation. Most difficult conversations go badly because people enter them without preparation, driven by emotion rather than structure.

## Inputs

- `situation-description`: What happened and why this conversation is needed
- `desired-outcome`: What you want to be true after the conversation that is not true now

## Outputs

- `your-interests`: Your underlying interests (not your position): what you actually need, why you need it, and what flexibility you have
- `their-likely-interests`: Your best estimate of the other person's interests: what they need, why, and what flexibility they might have
- `preparation-notes`: The facts (what happened, observable behavior, measurable impact), your interests, their likely interests, and options you could propose

## Process

1. Write down the facts: what specific, observable events occurred? Strip out interpretation and emotion. 'You missed the deadline by 5 days' is a fact. 'You do not care about deadlines' is an interpretation.
2. Identify your interests (not your position). Your position is what you want ('I want you to apologize'). Your interest is why you want it ('I need to know this will not happen again because it affects the client relationship'). Interests have more room for creative solutions than positions.
3. Estimate their interests: what are they likely concerned about? What constraints are they operating under? What do they need from you? If you cannot imagine their perspective, you are not ready for the conversation.
4. Draft 2-3 possible options that address both sets of interests. Enter the conversation with options, not demands.
