# Range Interpretation And Decision

Interpret the price range and decide where within it to set the actual price, based on positioning strategy.

## Inputs

- `intersection-points`: The Van Westendorp intersection points
- `product-description`: The product being priced

## Outputs

- `recommended-price`: The recommended price with the rationale for where it sits within the acceptable range
- `pricing-rationale`: Why this specific price was chosen (premium positioning, market penetration, competitor alignment, etc.)

## Process

1. The acceptable range (Marginal Cheapness to Marginal Expensiveness) is where you should price. Below this range, prospects question quality. Above it, they refuse to consider.
2. Where within the range depends on strategy: price at the high end for premium positioning (fewer customers, higher margins), at the low end for market penetration (more customers, lower margins), or at the Optimal Price Point for minimum resistance.
3. Compare the range to competitor pricing. If competitors are priced below your acceptable range, your product has perceived value differentiation. If they are within the range, you need other differentiation.
4. Set the price and document the rationale. The Van Westendorp data gives you empirical backing for the price, which is useful for internal debates and investor conversations.
