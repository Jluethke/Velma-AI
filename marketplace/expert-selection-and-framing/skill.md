# Expert Selection And Framing

Select the expert and frame the interview so they understand you are extracting tacit knowledge, not testing their ability.

## Inputs

- `expertise-domain`: The domain of expertise you want to transfer (medical diagnosis, negotiation, project estimation, debugging, sales, etc.)
- `expert-candidates`: People who have demonstrated expertise in this domain through consistent high performance

## Outputs

- `selected-expert`: The expert chosen for the interview, with the reason they were selected
- `interview-framing`: The explanation you will give the expert: what you are doing, why, and what kind of stories you need from them

## Process

1. Select an expert based on demonstrated performance, not title or tenure. The best expert is someone who consistently makes correct non-obvious decisions in ambiguous situations.
2. Frame the interview: 'I want to learn how you make decisions in [domain]. I am going to ask you to walk me through specific cases where you made a judgment call that turned out to be right. I am not testing you; I am trying to understand the thinking behind your decisions so I can learn from it.'
3. Schedule 60-90 minutes in a quiet setting. The interview requires concentration and memory retrieval, so avoid interruptions.
4. Prepare to record (with permission) or take detailed notes. The specific words the expert uses are important because they reveal their mental categories.
