# Social Environment Design

Redesign the social environment: surround yourself with people who model the desired behavior and reduce exposure to people who model the undesired behavior.

## Inputs

- `increase-behaviors`: Behaviors to do more
- `decrease-behaviors`: Behaviors to do less

## Outputs

- `social-changes`: Specific changes to social environment: groups to join, people to spend more time with, contexts to avoid
- `accountability-setup`: One accountability partner or group for the most important behavior change

## Process

1. Identify people in your life who already do the behavior you want to adopt. Spend more time with them in the context where they do that behavior. You absorb behavior from your social environment without conscious effort.
2. Identify contexts where the undesired behavior is the social norm. You do not need to cut off friendships, but you may need to change the context (meet at a coffee shop instead of a bar if you are reducing alcohol).
3. Find or create one accountability setup: a partner, a group, or a public commitment. The mechanism is simple: tell someone what you intend to do and agree to report back. The social pressure adds friction to skipping.
4. The social environment is the most effective and most overlooked design lever. A person who joins a running group runs more than a person who buys running shoes.
