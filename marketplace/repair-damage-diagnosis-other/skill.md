# Repair Damage Diagnosis Other

Diagnose the damage from the other person's perspective, genuinely reconstructing how the events looked and felt to them, because repair that addresses only your version of what happened misses the harm they actually experienced, and the gap between the two perspectives is usually where the repair lives.

## Inputs

- `self-perspective-diagnosis`: Your honest account of what happened and the damage
- `self-contribution-notes`: Your specific contributions to the damage

## Outputs

- `other-perspective-diagnosis`: A genuine reconstruction of how the events looked and felt from the other person's side
- `perspective-gap-map`: Where your account and their likely account diverge, which is where the real repair work sits

## Process

1. Reconstruct the events as they likely experienced them, not as you need them to have experienced them. Ask what they saw, what they felt, and what they concluded about you, drawing on what you know of them.
2. Take their harm seriously even where you think it is disproportionate. Whether their reaction seems fair is beside the point for repair, the point is that the harm is real to them and the relationship will not recover until it is addressed.
3. Map the gap between the two perspectives. Often each person is reacting to something the other does not even see, and naming that gap (you were hurt by X, which I did not realize mattered) is frequently the heart of the repair.
4. Where you genuinely cannot reconstruct their view, note it as something to learn rather than guess. The next step asks them directly, and an honest I am not sure how this looked to you beats a confident wrong assumption.
