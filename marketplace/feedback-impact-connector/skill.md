# Feedback Impact Connector

Connect the behavior to its impact. Impact is what makes feedback useful instead of annoying. Without impact, feedback is 'I noticed a thing.' With impact, feedback is 'this thing caused a problem.'

## Inputs

- `behavior-description`: What the person did
- `recipient-context`: The person's role and what they care about

## Outputs

- `impact-statement`: The specific consequence of the behavior on the work, the team, the relationship, or the outcome
- `impact-evidence`: Observable evidence of the impact (not your feeling about it, but the measurable result)

## Process

1. Name the impact: 'The presenter lost her place and the client asked to move on, so we did not cover the pricing section.'
2. Connect impact to something the recipient cares about. If they care about client relationships, lead with the client impact.
3. Use 'I' language for emotional impact: 'I felt the meeting went off track' not 'You derailed the meeting.'
4. Keep impact to 1-2 sentences. Impact that takes a paragraph to explain is probably multiple feedback items. Separate them.
