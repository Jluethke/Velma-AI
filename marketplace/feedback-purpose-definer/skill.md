# Feedback Purpose Definer

Define what the feedback system must accomplish. Most feedback systems fail because they try to do everything (measure, motivate, develop, rank) with one instrument.

## Inputs

- `context`: Where the feedback system will operate (a team, a company, a classroom, a personal relationship, a product)
- `desired-outcomes`: What should change as a result of the feedback (behavior change, skill development, product improvement, relationship improvement)

## Outputs

- `feedback-purpose`: The single primary purpose of the feedback system and the secondary purposes it must also serve
- `feedback-scope`: What is in scope (specific behaviors and their impacts) and what is out of scope (personality, intent, permanent traits)

## Process

1. Define the primary purpose: is this feedback for development (helping someone improve), evaluation (measuring performance), recognition (acknowledging contribution), or course correction (stopping a problem)?
2. Define the scope: feedback addresses behaviors and their impacts, not personality traits or intentions. 'You arrived late to the last three client meetings' is in scope. 'You are unprofessional' is out of scope.
3. Identify the feedback loops: who gives feedback to whom, how often, and through what channel?
4. Define what success looks like: the feedback system is working when recipients change specific behaviors in response to specific feedback, measurably.
