# Density Before Breadth Planner

Build density in a small market before expanding broadly. Network effects are strongest in concentrated networks and weakest in sparse ones.

## Inputs

- `network-effect-type`: The type and strength of the network effect
- `product-description`: The product context

## Outputs

- `target-market-selection`: The initial market to dominate: small enough to achieve density quickly, valuable enough to sustain the business
- `density-strategy`: How to achieve density: geographic focus, niche focus, community focus, or use-case focus

## Process

1. Select the smallest market where density creates a self-sustaining loop. For a marketplace: a single city or category. For a social product: a single community or organization. For a data product: a single use case.
2. Define density: what user or supply count makes the product clearly valuable in this market? For a marketplace, it might be 50 active sellers in one category. For a social product, it might be 200 active users in one community.
3. Focus all acquisition efforts on reaching density in this market before expanding. Spreading across multiple markets creates sparse networks where no user sees enough value.
4. The sign that density is achieved: organic growth in the target market (users invite users without paid acquisition) and retention is stable.
