# Emotional Acknowledgment

Acknowledge the other person's emotions before discussing the problem.

## Inputs

- `conflict-situation`: What the conflict is about
- `other-person-state`: How the other person seems to be feeling

## Outputs

- `acknowledgment-statement`: A statement that names and validates their emotion without agreeing with their position
- `emotional-temperature`: Assessment: is the person ready to problem-solve or do they need more acknowledgment?

## Process

1. Name the emotion you observe: 'You seem frustrated.' Naming an emotion reduces its intensity. Research shows that labeling emotions activates the prefrontal cortex and calms the amygdala.
2. Validate without agreeing: 'I understand why you would feel that way given what happened' does not mean 'You are right.' It means 'Your emotional response makes sense.'
3. Do not skip to problem-solving. Logic bounces off an activated emotional state.
4. Check the temperature: after acknowledging, does the person relax slightly? If yes, move to problem-solving. If no, continue acknowledging.
