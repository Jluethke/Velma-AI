# Trust Portfolio Assessor

Assess your entire trust portfolio: how your trust is distributed across contacts and whether the distribution matches your strategic needs.

## Inputs

- `failure-protocols`: Protocols for trust failures
- `recovery-pathways`: When and how to recover from trust failures

## Outputs

- `trust-distribution`: Count of contacts at each trust level and whether the distribution is healthy (pyramid shape: many at level 1, fewer at each higher level)
- `trust-concentration-risks`: Areas where your high-trust relationships are concentrated in a single organization, industry, or social circle

## Process

1. Count contacts at each trust level. A healthy distribution is a pyramid: many at levels 0-1, fewer at level 2, a small group at level 3, and a handful at level 4. If the distribution is inverted (many at level 3-4, few at level 1), you are not building new relationships. If it is flat (roughly equal at all levels), you may be escalating trust too quickly.
2. Assess concentration risk. If all your level 3-4 contacts are in one organization, a single organizational event (layoff, restructuring, acquisition) could wipe out your high-trust network. If all are in one industry, an industry downturn affects everyone simultaneously. Diversify high-trust relationships across contexts.
3. Identify the trust bottleneck: the level where the most relationships stall. If many contacts are stuck at level 1 (informational but never advancing to reputational), you may not be making enough escalation moves. If many stall at level 2, the reciprocity test at level 3 may be too demanding or you may not be offering enough to justify the escalation.
4. Produce the quarterly trust portfolio review: changes in trust distribution, notable escalations and downgrades, concentration risk changes, and 3-5 relationships to prioritize for escalation in the next quarter.
