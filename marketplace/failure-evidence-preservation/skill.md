# Failure Evidence Preservation

Capture and protect the as-found evidence before any handling destroys it, and reconstruct the timeline and loading at failure.

## Inputs

- `failed-hardware`: The failed part or assembly and its surrounding components, as recovered
- `service-history`: The operating, load, and environmental history, plus the event description at the time of failure

## Outputs

- `evidence-record`: The documented as-found condition: photographs, fragment map, chain of custody, and preserved fracture surfaces
- `failure-timeline`: The reconstructed sequence and loading at failure, with the operating point and environment at the moment of failure

## Process

1. Photograph and map before touching anything. Record the as-found position of every fragment, the mating surfaces, and the fracture faces at multiple scales before disassembly. A fracture surface rubbed clean or two fragments fitted back together by hand can destroy the markings that identify the origin and direction.
2. Protect the fracture surfaces from contamination and corrosion. Do not touch the fracture face, do not fit pieces together, and keep the surfaces dry and clean; package fragments individually. Fingerprint oils, handling debris, and a few hours of humidity can obscure fatigue striations and the origin.
3. Establish chain of custody and label every fragment. In a failure that may reach a formal review or dispute, who held the part and when matters as much as the metallurgy. Bag, label, and log each piece with its recovery location.
4. Reconstruct the loading and environment at failure from records, not memory. Pull the load spectrum, temperature, pressure, and operating point at the failure instant from data logs and the event report. Note what was nominal and what was off-nominal; the off-nominal condition is often the trigger.
5. Separate the primary failure from secondary damage up front. In an assembly, one part fails first and others break as a consequence. Identify candidate primary fractures (often the one with the oldest-looking surface or the origin that fits the load path) so the investigation targets the cause, not a downstream symptom.
