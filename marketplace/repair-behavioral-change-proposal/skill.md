# Repair Behavioral Change Proposal

Propose concrete behavioral changes that address what they said they need, because trust is rebuilt through changed behavior over time, not through words, and a vague promise to do better gives them nothing to observe, while a specific change gives them something they can actually watch for.

## Inputs

- `repair-definition-questions`: What the other person said repair would require
- `listening-protocol`: How their answer was received

## Outputs

- `behavioral-change-proposals`: Specific, observable changes in your behavior that address what they identified as needed
- `change-commitments`: The changes stated as commitments you can be held to, with enough specificity that they will be able to tell whether you kept them

## Process

1. Translate what they need into specific behaviors. Not I will be more reliable but I will confirm commitments in writing and tell you immediately if something is at risk. A change they can observe is worth more than a virtue they have to take on faith.
2. Propose changes that genuinely address their stated need, not changes that are easy for you but miss the point. If they needed to feel consulted, the change is about how you include them, not an unrelated gesture.
3. Commit only to changes you will actually sustain, because a repair promise you break confirms their worst conclusion and does more damage than the original injury. Under-promise and hold it rather than over-promise and fail.
4. State the commitments specifically enough to be checkable, so both of you know what keeping them looks like. Vague commitments cannot be observed, which means they cannot rebuild trust, because trust rebuilds precisely by them watching you keep a specific word.
