# Stake Position Classification

Classify each stakeholder's current position toward the outcome as supportive, neutral, or opposed, and rate your confidence, because position is often misread (public agreement can mask private opposition) and acting on a wrong read wastes effort or triggers an ambush.

## Inputs

- `stakeholder-list`: Everyone who affects or is affected by the outcome
- `stakeholder-roles`: Each stakeholder's role relative to the decision

## Outputs

- `position-map`: Each stakeholder marked supportive, neutral, or opposed toward the outcome you want
- `position-confidence`: How confident you are in each read, flagging the ones based on assumption rather than evidence

## Process

1. Mark each stakeholder supportive, neutral, or opposed based on what you actually know, not on what their role suggests they should think. People surprise you in both directions.
2. Rate your confidence in each read. A position you have heard them state is high confidence, a position you are inferring from their incentives is a guess, and guesses need verification before you build a plan on them.
3. Watch for the gap between public and private positions. Someone agreeable in meetings may be privately opposed and working against the effort quietly, which is far more dangerous than open opposition.
4. Flag the low-confidence reads for direct checking. Where a stakeholder is important and your read is a guess, finding out their real position (through conversation or a trusted intermediary) is worth doing before you plan around an assumption.
