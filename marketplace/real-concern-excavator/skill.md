# Real Concern Excavator

For each stated objection, find the real concern underneath it. 'Too expensive' means 'I do not trust the return.' 'Not the right time' means 'I do not want to own the risk.' The stated objection is the symptom. The real concern is the diagnosis.

## Inputs

- `stated-objections`: What people say when they object
- `stakeholder-exposure-map`: What each stakeholder actually loses

## Outputs

- `concern-pairs`: For each stated objection: the stated version, the real concern, and the evidence for why the real concern is what it is
- `concern-clusters`: Objections grouped by their shared real concern

## Process

1. For each stated objection, apply the 'what would change their mind' test: if I addressed this objection perfectly, would they agree? If not, the stated objection is not the real concern.
2. Map the stated objection to the stakeholder's exposure. The real concern almost always maps to the biggest thing they lose.
3. Group objections that share the same underlying concern. Five different stated objections from one stakeholder often trace to one real concern.
4. Write the real concern in plain language. No jargon, no softening. 'They are afraid they will be blamed if this fails' is a real concern.
