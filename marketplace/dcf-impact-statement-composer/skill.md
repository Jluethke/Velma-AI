# Dcf Impact Statement Composer

State the impact of what happened without attributing intent. You can know the impact on you with certainty. You cannot know their intent. Confusing the two turns a describable harm into an accusation they will deny.

## Inputs

- `contribution-map`: How each side contributed to the situation
- `feelings-inventory`: The feelings that the impact produced in you

## Outputs

- `impact-statements`: Sentences describing the effect on you, separated from any claim about why they acted
- `intent-separation-notes`: The intent claims you removed and why each one would derail the conversation

## Process

1. Write the impact as 'When X happened, the effect on me was Y.' Keep Y to observable consequences and named feelings.
2. Strike every clause that asserts their intent. 'You ignored me to make me look bad' claims a motive. 'When I did not hear back, I was left unsure where the project stood' states the impact.
3. Where you suspect intent, convert it into a curiosity question for later, not a statement now.
4. Read each impact statement aloud and check it against one test: could they agree this was the effect, even while disagreeing about why? If not, it still contains a hidden accusation.
