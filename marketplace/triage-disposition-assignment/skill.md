# Triage Disposition Assignment

Combine the three answers into a disposition for each relationship: keep (invest fully), maintain with boundaries (continue in a limited, defined way), or let go gracefully. The middle option matters most, since most relationships are not clean keeps or clean cuts and deserve a defined, limited place rather than an all-or-nothing verdict.

## Inputs

- `alignment-assessments`: Alignment reading per relationship
- `alignment-conflicts`: Positive relationships that conflict with your direction

## Outputs

- `disposition-assignments`: Each relationship assigned keep, maintain with boundaries, or let go gracefully, based on the three questions together
- `boundary-notes`: For the maintain-with-boundaries group, the specific limits that let the relationship continue without the cost that triggered the flag

## Process

1. Combine the three readings. A relationship that gives energy, is mutual, and aligns is a clear keep. One that drains, is one-sided, and misaligns is a clear let-go. Most relationships land somewhere between, which is where the middle option does its work.
2. Assign maintain-with-boundaries to the mixed cases: the family member you will not cut but whose draining patterns you will limit, the old friend you will see occasionally but no longer center, the alignment conflict you keep in a smaller role.
3. For each maintain-with-boundaries relationship, define the actual boundary: how often, in what contexts, with what topics off limits, with what level of investment. A boundary that is only a vague intention does not hold.
4. Resist resolving every hard case as a cut. Letting go is right for some, but the maintain-with-boundaries option preserves relationships that are worth a limited place, which an all-or-nothing triage would wrongly destroy.
