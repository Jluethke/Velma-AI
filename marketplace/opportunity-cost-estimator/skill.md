# Opportunity Cost Estimator

Estimate the opportunity cost: what else could the money and time produce if redirected to the next-best alternative?

## Inputs

- `direct-costs`: Direct costs of the subject
- `time-cost`: Time costs of the subject

## Outputs

- `opportunity-cost`: The value of the next-best alternative use of the same money and time, expressed as a monthly or per-unit amount
- `alternative-comparison`: The specific alternative considered, what it would produce, and why it was chosen as the benchmark

## Process

1. Identify the next-best alternative for the money. If you weren't spending $200/month on this, what would you do with $200/month? Invest it (calculate the expected return), pay down debt (the interest saved), or fund a different activity (its value).
2. Identify the next-best alternative for the time. If you weren't spending 5 hours/month on this, what would you do with 5 hours? Bill it to a client (at your hourly rate), invest it in a skill (estimated future value), or use it for recovery (reduced burnout cost).
3. The opportunity cost is the value of the better of the two alternatives (money or time), not both added together, because the alternatives compete for different resources.
4. Be honest about the alternative. If the money would realistically sit in a checking account earning nothing, the financial opportunity cost is near zero. If the time would realistically be spent on social media, the time opportunity cost is near zero. Opportunity cost only applies to a credible alternative.
