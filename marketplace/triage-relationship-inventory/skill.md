# Triage Relationship Inventory

Inventory the relationships to triage and capture how the life change has already shifted each one, because a major change tends to rearrange relationships on its own (some deepen, some strain, some lose their shared basis) and the triage works with that movement rather than against it.

## Inputs

- `life-change-context`: The life change prompting the triage and how it has altered your circumstances, priorities, and available time
- `relationship-pool`: The relationships currently in your life, across personal, family, social, and professional spheres

## Outputs

- `triage-inventory`: The relationships to triage, with the ones most affected by the change noted
- `change-impact-notes`: How the life change has already shifted each relationship: strengthened, strained, or lost its shared basis

## Process

1. List the relationships that are active or lingering in your life right now across every sphere, including the ones that feel obligatory and the ones running purely on momentum.
2. Note how the change has affected each. A move strains proximity-based friendships, a job change dissolves relationships built only on shared work, a loss or a health event reveals who shows up. The change has already begun sorting them.
3. Mark the relationships most disrupted by the change, because those are where conscious triage matters most. Relationships untouched by the change can mostly continue as they are.
4. Approach the inventory without guilt. A life change legitimately reshapes a social world, and some relationships ending is natural rather than a failure. The triage names that honestly instead of letting it happen by neglect.
