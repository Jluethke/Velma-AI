# Cccm Eight Dimensions Primer

Learn the eight dimensions of the Culture Map well enough to place a conversation on them. The dimensions cover how people communicate, give negative feedback, persuade, lead, decide, build trust, disagree, and treat time. Each is a spectrum, and cultures sit at different points, not at a right answer.

## Inputs

- `communication-setting`: The interaction you are preparing for: a negotiation, a team you lead, a client relationship
- `counterpart-culture`: The cultural background of the people you are communicating with

## Outputs

- `dimension-definitions`: The eight dimensions defined as spectrums, each with its two poles described plainly
- `setting-summary`: A short summary of the interaction and which dimensions are likely to matter most in it

## Process

1. Write the eight dimensions as spectrums: communicating from low-context to high-context, evaluating from direct to indirect negative feedback, persuading from principles-first to applications-first, leading from egalitarian to hierarchical, deciding from consensual to top-down, trusting from task-based to relationship-based, disagreeing from confrontational to confrontation-avoiding, and scheduling from linear time to flexible time.
2. For each dimension, describe both poles in plain terms so you can recognize them in behavior, not just name them.
3. Summarize the interaction you are preparing for, and flag which dimensions are most likely to shape it. A negotiation leans on trusting and disagreeing, a project handoff on scheduling and deciding.
4. Remember the dimensions are relative. A culture is direct or indirect only compared with another, so the comparison is what matters, not the label.
