# Trust Ledger Deposit Withdrawal Taxonomy

Define what actually counts as a deposit and a withdrawal in each context, because an abstract model is useless until you can name the concrete behaviors that build or spend trust with these specific people.

## Inputs

- `top-relationships`: The 20 relationships on the ledger
- `context-tags`: The context of each relationship

## Outputs

- `deposit-catalog`: The concrete deposit behaviors per context: keeping commitments, clarifying expectations, small kindnesses, loyalty in their absence, sincere apologies
- `withdrawal-catalog`: The concrete withdrawal behaviors per context: broken promises, unclear expectations, unkindness, criticism behind their back, requests and favors

## Process

1. Start from Covey's six deposit types (keeping commitments, clarifying expectations, attending to small things, showing personal integrity and loyalty to the absent, delivering sincere apologies when you withdraw, understanding the individual) and translate each into a concrete behavior for your contexts.
2. For deposits, write what it looks like with these people specifically. In a manager relationship, a deposit is defending your report in a meeting they are not in. In a client relationship, it is flagging a problem before they notice it.
3. For withdrawals, be honest about the ordinary ones. Asking for a favor is a withdrawal even when granted. Delivering bad news is a withdrawal even when necessary. A withdrawal is not a wrong, it is a draw on the balance.
4. Note the asymmetry: in most relationships a withdrawal spends more than a deposit builds, so trust is slow to accumulate and fast to drain. Your catalogs should reflect that a broken promise can undo many small kindnesses.
