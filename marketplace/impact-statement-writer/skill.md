# Impact Statement Writer

Write the impact: what the observation caused. Impact is on you, the team, the relationship, or the outcome. Impact is felt, not judged. 'When you missed the deadline, the client escalated to the VP' not 'You are unreliable.'

## Inputs

- `observation-statement`: The factual description of what happened
- `their-stakes`: What the other person stands to lose, so the impact statement addresses what they care about

## Outputs

- `impact-statement`: The specific consequences of the observed behavior, stated without judgment
- `emotional-impact-note`: How the situation made you feel, stated as 'I felt X' not 'You made me feel X'

## Process

1. State the concrete consequences: what happened as a result of the behavior. Lost money, lost time, damaged relationship, missed opportunity.
2. State the emotional impact using 'I' language: 'I felt unsupported' not 'You were unsupportive.'
3. Connect the impact to something the other person cares about, using their stakes. If they care about the client, lead with the client impact.
4. Keep the impact statement to 2-3 sentences. Longer impact statements feel like an attack, not information.
