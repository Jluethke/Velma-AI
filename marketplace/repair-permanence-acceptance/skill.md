# Repair Permanence Acceptance

Assess honestly whether the relationship can fully recover and accept that some damage is permanent, because not every relationship returns to what it was, and a mature repair includes recognizing when the realistic outcome is a changed relationship, a reduced one, or a respectful ending rather than a full restoration.

## Inputs

- `followthrough-plan`: The plan to sustain the changes over time
- `visibility-markers`: How kept commitments become observable to the other person

## Outputs

- `permanence-assessment`: An honest read of how fully the relationship can recover, given their response and the nature of the damage
- `acceptance-or-closure-plan`: How to accept the realistic outcome: a restored relationship, a changed or reduced one, or a respectful ending

## Process

1. After genuine follow-through over time, read the realistic ceiling of the recovery from their response, not from your hope. Some relationships return fully, some settle into a changed form, some reduce to civil distance, and some cannot continue.
2. Accept that some damage is permanent without treating that as failure. You can do the repair perfectly and still not get the relationship back, because the other person has their own agency and some injuries change a relationship for good.
3. Match your acceptance to the realistic outcome. If it recovered, invest in the changed relationship on its new footing. If it reduced, hold it at the level it can sustain without forcing more. If it ended, let it end with the ownership intact and without demanding absolution.
4. Take the durable gain regardless of outcome: you owned your part, you changed real behavior, and you learned what the damage was. That stands even if the relationship does not, and it is what you carry into every relationship that follows.
