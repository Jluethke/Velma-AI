# Fractographic Examination

Examine the fracture surface using optical and scanning electron microscopy to identify the micro-scale failure mechanism.

## Inputs

- `documented-evidence`: Preserved fracture surfaces with identified origin area
- `initial-hypothesis`: Preliminary failure mechanism to guide the examination

## Outputs

- `fracture-mechanism`: Identified microscopic fracture mechanism (transgranular cleavage, intergranular, microvoid coalescence, fatigue striations, quasi-cleavage) with supporting SEM images
- `origin-characterization`: Nature of the fracture origin (inclusion, pit, machining defect, hydrogen flake, pre-existing crack) with elemental analysis if applicable

## Process

1. Clean the fracture surface in stages. Start with compressed air to remove loose debris. Then ultrasonic cleaning in acetone or isopropanol. For corrosion products that must be preserved for analysis, clean only a portion of the fracture surface and leave the rest undisturbed. For surfaces heavily covered in corrosion or contamination, try replica stripping (cellulose acetate tape) before aggressive cleaning.
2. Examine in the SEM at increasing magnification. Start at low magnification (20-50x) to map the entire fracture surface and locate the origin area. Then increase to 200-500x to characterize the fracture morphology in the origin region, the propagation zone, and the final fracture zone. At 1000-5000x, identify the microscopic mechanism.
3. Interpret the fracture morphology. Dimples (microvoid coalescence) = ductile fracture. Equiaxed dimples = tensile overload. Elongated dimples = shear overload. Cleavage facets with river lines = brittle transgranular fracture. Smooth intergranular facets = grain boundary weakening (temper embrittlement, hydrogen embrittlement, creep, SCC). Fatigue striations = cyclic loading (spacing corresponds to crack growth rate per cycle). Quasi-cleavage = mixed mechanism typical of high-strength steels or hydrogen-assisted fracture.
4. At the origin, use energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) to characterize any inclusions or foreign material. MnS inclusions (Mn + S signal) are common fatigue initiation sites in steel. Oxide inclusions (Al + O) from the steelmaking process. Corrosion pits show chloride or sulfur. Fretting debris shows oxidized base metal particles. The EDS finding links the fracture origin to the root cause.
