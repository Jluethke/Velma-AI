# Netgap Cluster Mapping

Map your network into clusters by shared context (employer, industry, school, geography, community), because the structural view that reveals gaps requires seeing your network as a set of groups rather than a flat list of individuals.

## Inputs

- `contact-list`: Your contacts with enough metadata to group them: how you know them, their industry, role, and location
- `clustering-basis`: The dimensions to cluster on: shared employer, industry, profession, school, geography, social community

## Outputs

- `cluster-map`: Your network grouped into clusters, each a set of contacts who share a context and who mostly know each other
- `cluster-labels`: A label and rough size for each cluster, with a note on how connected its members are to each other

## Process

1. Group your contacts by shared context. Most people cluster naturally: current coworkers, former coworkers, an industry group, a school cohort, a geographic community, a hobby or social circle.
2. Label each cluster and estimate its size. The size matters because a very large cluster is where you are probably over-invested, and a thin cluster may be a bridge worth strengthening.
3. Note internal density, how much the members of a cluster know each other. A dense cluster (everyone knows everyone) gives you redundant information, because anything one member knows the others likely know too.
4. Identify which clusters you personally bridge, where you are the main link between two groups. Those bridges are a source of your value, and they are also fragile, which later steps address.
