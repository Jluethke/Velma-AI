# Dcf Feelings Acknowledgment Plan

Plan how to handle the feelings strand. Feelings are to be acknowledged, not debated. When someone feels unheard, no amount of logic moves them. Acknowledgment is not agreement, it is proof you understood.

## Inputs

- `feelings-conversation`: The emotions present on both sides
- `contribution-map`: How each side contributed, which often explains the feelings

## Outputs

- `feelings-inventory`: The feelings you will name openly and the ones you will hold back for now
- `acknowledgment-statements`: Sentences that show you understood their feeling without conceding the facts

## Process

1. List your own feelings and decide which to state directly. Some belong in the conversation, some belong in your own preparation only.
2. For each feeling you expect from them, write an acknowledgment sentence of the form 'It makes sense you would feel X given Y.' This names the feeling and its cause without ruling on whether the facts support it.
3. Separate acknowledgment from agreement in your own notes, so that under pressure you do not slide from 'I hear that you are frustrated' into 'you are right to be frustrated' before you mean to.
4. Decide what you will do if feelings escalate: name the escalation out loud, slow down, and return to acknowledgment before continuing.
