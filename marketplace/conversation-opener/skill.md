# Conversation Opener

Open the conversation by stating the purpose, acknowledging both perspectives, and inviting their view first.

## Inputs

- `preparation-notes`: The prepared facts, interests, and options
- `desired-outcome`: What you want to be true after the conversation

## Outputs

- `opening-script`: The first three sentences of the conversation: the purpose, the acknowledgment of complexity, and the invitation to share their perspective
- `ground-rules`: The ground rules for the conversation: no interrupting, focus on interests, no attribution of motive

## Process

1. State the purpose neutrally: 'I want to talk about [the situation] because [it matters to the relationship/project/team]. I have some observations and I want to hear your perspective too.'
2. Acknowledge the complexity: 'I recognize this is not simple and there may be things I do not see.' This is not weakness; it is accuracy. You do not have the full picture.
3. Invite their view first: 'Before I share my perspective, I would like to hear yours. What happened from your side?' Listening first accomplishes two things: you learn information you did not have, and the other person feels heard (which makes them more receptive).
4. Set the ground rule: 'I would like us to focus on what we both need and what we can do going forward, not on assigning blame.'
