# Referral Ask Integrator

Build the referral ask into natural conversation touchpoints so it does not feel like a sales pitch or an imposition.

## Inputs

- `motivation-map`: Referrer motivations by type
- `anti-motivations`: What discourages referrals

## Outputs

- `ask-touchpoints`: The specific moments in a relationship where a referral ask is natural and welcome (after delivering value, after receiving a compliment, after solving a problem)
- `ask-scripts`: Word-for-word ask templates for each touchpoint, calibrated to the referrer's motivation

## Process

1. Identify the 3 natural ask touchpoints. (1) After delivering value: you just solved a problem, shipped a project, or helped them with something, and they expressed satisfaction. (2) After receiving unsolicited praise: they told someone else you did good work, or they gave you a testimonial unprompted. (3) After a milestone: a contract renewal, a project completion, an anniversary of working together. These moments have high trust and positive sentiment.
2. Design the ask for each touchpoint. After delivering value: 'I am glad that worked out. Is there anyone else you know dealing with [specific problem] who might benefit from the same approach?' After praise: 'That means a lot. Would you be comfortable saying that to [specific person or in specific context]?' After a milestone: 'We have been working together for [time], and it has been productive. Who else in your network faces similar challenges?'
3. The ask must be specific. 'Do you know anyone who might need help?' fails because it requires the referrer to do the work of matching. 'Do you know any operations managers at mid-size logistics companies dealing with route optimization?' succeeds because the referrer can immediately think of a specific person.
4. Build a do-not-ask list: contacts who have declined a referral ask, contacts where the relationship is too new (below trust level 2), and contacts who are in a difficult period (layoff, personal crisis). Asking at the wrong moment burns social capital faster than not asking at all.
