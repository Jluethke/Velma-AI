# Sunk Cost Separator

Separate sunk costs from opportunity costs. Sunk costs are already spent and irrelevant to the forward-looking decision. Only future costs and opportunity costs matter.

## Inputs

- `true-cost-calculation`: The true cost analysis
- `proposed-use`: The proposed allocation

## Outputs

- `forward-looking-analysis`: The decision stripped of sunk costs: only future direct costs, future opportunity costs, and future benefits
- `sunk-cost-inventory`: The sunk costs identified and explicitly excluded from the decision, with the reasoning

## Process

1. Identify any costs already incurred: money already spent, time already invested, contracts already signed. These are sunk costs.
2. Remove them from the analysis. The question is never 'should I have started this?' but 'given where I am now, should I continue?'
3. Recalculate the forward-looking true cost: remaining direct costs plus the opportunity cost of the remaining resources.
4. The most common error: continuing a losing investment because 'we have already spent too much to stop.' The sunk cost is gone regardless. The only question is whether the remaining investment produces more than its opportunity cost.
