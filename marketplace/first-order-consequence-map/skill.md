# First Order Consequence Map

For each option, list the immediate, direct consequences that will happen in the first days or weeks.

## Inputs

- `framed-decision`: The clear decision statement
- `option-list`: All options including do-nothing

## Outputs

- `first-order-consequences`: For each option: 3-5 immediate consequences (things that will happen directly and quickly as a result of choosing this option)
- `first-order-confidence`: Confidence level for each consequence (high = very likely, medium = probable, low = possible)

## Process

1. For each option, ask: 'If I choose this, what happens immediately?' List 3-5 direct consequences.
2. First-order consequences are the ones most people already think about. They are obvious: 'If we raise the price, some customers will leave.' This is the easy part.
3. Rate your confidence in each consequence. High confidence means you have evidence or experience. Low confidence means you are guessing.
4. Note: first-order consequences are necessary but insufficient for good decisions. The interesting effects are at the second and third order.
