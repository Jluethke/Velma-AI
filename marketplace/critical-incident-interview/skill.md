# Critical Incident Interview

Interview knowledge holders using the critical incident technique. Do not ask them to explain their process. Ask them to describe specific times they made a non-obvious judgment call.

## Inputs

- `knowledge-holder-map`: Map of who knows what
- `interview-priority-list`: Prioritized list of interviewees

## Outputs

- `incident-transcripts`: Transcripts of critical incident interviews organized by knowledge domain
- `judgment-pattern-notes`: Notes on recurring patterns: cues, heuristics, and rules of thumb that experts use but have never articulated

## Process

1. The opening question: 'Tell me about a time in the last year when you had to make a judgment call that a less experienced person would have gotten wrong.' Let them tell the story without interrupting.
2. Follow-up probes: 'What was the first thing you noticed that told you something was off?' (cues). 'What options did you consider?' (mental model). 'Why did you pick that one?' (reasoning). 'What would a less experienced person have done instead?' (expertise gap).
3. Do not ask 'how do you do X?' That produces procedures, not expertise. Procedures describe the happy path. Critical incidents reveal the edge cases, the exceptions, and the judgment calls.
4. Each interview is 45 minutes. Plan 4 to 6 interviews per knowledge holder, each covering a different domain of their expertise. Space interviews one week apart to avoid fatigue.
