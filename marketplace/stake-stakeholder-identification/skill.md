# Stake Stakeholder Identification

Identify everyone who affects or is affected by the decision or project, including the quiet influencers who hold no formal role but shape the outcome, because the stakeholder who sinks a project is often the one nobody put on the list.

## Inputs

- `decision-or-project`: The decision or project whose stakeholders you are mapping, and the outcome you want
- `scope-notes`: The boundaries of the effort: what it touches, whose work or interests it changes, and who holds approval or veto

## Outputs

- `stakeholder-list`: Everyone who affects or is affected by the outcome, formal and informal
- `stakeholder-roles`: Each stakeholder's relationship to the decision: decision-maker, approver, influencer, implementer, or affected party

## Process

1. List the obvious stakeholders first: the formal decision-makers, approvers, and the people whose work the outcome directly changes.
2. Then hunt for the non-obvious ones: the trusted adviser a decision-maker always consults, the veteran whose opinion carries weight informally, the team that will have to live with the result and can quietly undermine it.
3. Classify each by their relationship to the decision: who decides, who must approve, who influences the deciders, who implements, and who is simply affected. The role determines how much their position matters.
4. Check for the invisible veto. Many efforts fail because of a stakeholder with no formal authority but real informal power to block, so deliberately ask who could kill this quietly and make sure they are on the list.
