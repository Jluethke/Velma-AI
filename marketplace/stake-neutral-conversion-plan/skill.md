# Stake Neutral Conversion Plan

Build the plan to convert the neutrals first, because neutrals are the cheapest to move (they are not invested in opposing you), converting them builds visible momentum, and shifting the center of gravity toward support often makes the opposed far easier to handle afterward.

## Inputs

- `influence-network`: Who influences whom among stakeholders
- `influencer-ranking`: Stakeholders ranked by influence reach

## Outputs

- `neutral-conversion-plan`: The plan to move the neutrals to support, prioritizing high-influence neutrals and using each one's specific lever
- `conversion-sequence`: The order of approach, sequenced so early conversions build momentum and influence toward the harder ones

## Process

1. Start with the neutrals, not the loudest opponent. Neutrals are cheaper to move because they hold no stake in opposing you, and a visible run of neutrals turning supportive shifts the perceived consensus.
2. Prioritize high-influence neutrals using the influence map, because converting one neutral who sways three others is worth more than converting three isolated ones. Apply each neutral's specific lever from the lever step.
3. Sequence the approach so momentum compounds. Convert the accessible, high-influence neutrals first, let their visible support make the next ones easier, and build toward a tipping point where support looks like the default position.
4. Track the shifting center of gravity. As neutrals convert, the opposed increasingly find themselves isolated rather than leading a faction, which is exactly the condition under which the opposition-handling step becomes manageable.
