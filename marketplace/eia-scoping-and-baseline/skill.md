# Eia Scoping And Baseline

Define the scope of the environmental assessment and conduct baseline environmental surveys.

## Inputs

- `project-description`: Proposed project: location, footprint, construction methods, operational characteristics
- `regulatory-framework`: Applicable environmental regulations (NEPA, state CEQA, EU EIA Directive, etc.)

## Outputs

- `scoping-report`: Identified environmental topics to assess, study area boundaries, and assessment methodology for each topic
- `baseline-conditions`: Existing environmental conditions for each topic: ecology, air, noise, water, visual, cultural, socioeconomic

## Process

1. Screen the project. Determine if EIA is required (thresholds vary by jurisdiction: NEPA for federal action, state CEQA/SEPA for state involvement, EU EIA Directive Annex I mandatory / Annex II screening). Determine level of assessment: Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for significant impacts, Environmental Assessment (EA) for uncertain significance, Categorical Exclusion if clearly no impact.
2. Conduct scoping. Identify all environmental topics potentially affected: air quality, noise and vibration, water resources (surface and ground), ecology (habitats, species), landscape and visual, cultural heritage, traffic and transport, socioeconomic, human health, climate (greenhouse gas emissions), cumulative effects. Scoping consultation with regulatory agencies and public to identify key concerns. Define study area for each topic (noise: typically 500 m from works, ecology: wider for mobile species).
3. Conduct baseline surveys. Ecology: Phase 1 habitat survey (extended to UK NVC or equivalent classification), protected species surveys (bat, bird, reptile, amphibian, botanical) timed to survey seasons. Air quality: diffusion tube monitoring for NO2 (minimum 3 months, ideally 12), PM10/PM2.5 monitoring if near sensitive receptors. Noise: attended and unattended noise monitoring at representative receptor locations (minimum 1 week continuous, capturing weekday and weekend). Water: river flow and quality monitoring, groundwater level monitoring. Traffic: classified traffic counts on affected roads.
4. Document baseline. For each topic, describe existing conditions quantitatively where possible. Identify sensitive receptors (residential properties, schools, hospitals, protected habitats, listed buildings, watercourses). Map receptor locations and distances from the project. Identify any existing environmental issues (exceedances of air quality standards, degraded habitats, contaminated land).
5. Define assessment methodology for each topic. Significance = f(magnitude of impact, sensitivity of receptor). Use standardized matrices (e.g., IEMA guidelines, DMRB for transport). Define magnitude categories (negligible, minor, moderate, major) with quantitative thresholds where possible (e.g., noise: < 1 dB negligible, 1-3 dB minor, 3-5 dB moderate, > 5 dB major change). Define sensitivity categories for receptors.
