# Stress Test Action Plan

Produce the action plan: what to do now to improve resilience, and what contingency plans to have ready if a shock hits.

## Inputs

- `combined-stress-result`: The combined scenario results
- `financial-resilience-score`: The resilience score
- `income-shock-cut-list`: The cuts needed under income shock

## Outputs

- `resilience-improvement-plan`: Specific actions to take now to improve the resilience score: build reserves, reduce variable-rate exposure, diversify income, or reduce fixed obligations
- `stress-test-report`: The complete stress test report: baseline, three individual scenarios, combined scenario, resilience score, and action plan

## Process

1. Prioritize improvements by the scenario that causes the most damage. If income shock is the biggest threat, prioritize income diversification and reserve building. If expense shock, prioritize reserve building and insurance review. If rate shock, prioritize converting variable-rate debt to fixed.
2. Set a target resilience score: if currently below 25, target 50 within 12 months. If below 50, target 75 within 18 months. Calculate the specific reserve increase, income diversification, or debt restructuring needed to hit the target.
3. Write the contingency plan: a pre-decided list of actions to execute if each shock type occurs. Having the plan ready before the shock means you act in hours instead of panicking for weeks. The plan should be specific enough that someone else could execute it.
4. Produce the final report with all sections: baseline model, individual shock results, combined scenario, resilience score, improvement plan, contingency plans, and the schedule for the next stress test (recommended: semi-annually).
