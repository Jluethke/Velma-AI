# Psm Survey Designer

Design the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter survey with the four standard questions adapted to the specific product or service.

## Inputs

- `product-description`: The product or service to price, described in terms the target buyer understands
- `target-respondents`: Who to survey: existing customers, prospects, or a representative sample of the target market

## Outputs

- `psm-survey`: The four Van Westendorp questions customized for this product, with the price range to present and the response format
- `sample-plan`: How many responses are needed (minimum 30, ideal 100+), how to recruit respondents, and how to avoid bias

## Process

1. Adapt the four standard questions to this product: (1) At what price would you consider this product to be so cheap that you would question its quality? (2) At what price would you consider this product to be a bargain, a great value for the money? (3) At what price would you consider this product to be getting expensive, but you might still consider it? (4) At what price would you consider this product to be too expensive, no matter what?
2. Set the response format: open-ended dollar amounts (preferred, avoids anchoring) or a predefined price list (easier to analyze but anchors responses to the options). If using a predefined list, make it wide enough to capture the full range (2x below to 2x above your initial price estimate).
3. Plan the sample: minimum 30 respondents for a rough read, 100+ for statistically useful results. Recruit from the actual target buyer population, not friends and family. Avoid revealing your current price before the survey.
4. Add a disqualifier: if the respondent is not in the target market or has no purchase intent, exclude their responses. Price sensitivity data from non-buyers is noise.
