# Recip Relationship Selection

Select the relationships to map and define the value lens you will measure through, because reciprocity means different things in different relationships and a single money-only lens would misread a friendship that trades mostly in support and attention.

## Inputs

- `network-list`: The relationships you could map across personal and professional life
- `value-lens`: The kinds of value that count: time, help, money, introductions, emotional support, information, opportunities

## Outputs

- `focus-relationships`: The relationships to map, chosen where the exchange is significant enough that imbalance would matter
- `lens-definition`: The explicit definition of what counts as value in these relationships, so give and receive are measured consistently

## Process

1. Choose relationships where enough value flows that balance matters. A relationship with almost no exchange cannot be meaningfully imbalanced, so focus on the ones carrying real give and take.
2. Define the value lens explicitly and broadly. Value is not just favors or money, it includes time, attention, emotional support, information, introductions, and opportunities. A narrow lens makes healthy relationships look one-sided.
3. Match the lens to the relationship type. In a friendship, emotional support and presence dominate. In a professional relationship, information, introductions, and opportunities weigh more. Use the right lens for each.
4. Keep the set manageable, around 10 to 20 relationships. Mapping value flow honestly takes thought per relationship, and a bloated list gets filled in carelessly.
