# Aar Scope And Prep

Define the scope of the after-action review and prepare the facts. Gather the plan (what was supposed to happen) and the actual timeline (what did happen) before the review meeting.

## Inputs

- `event-description`: Description of the event, project, or incident to review
- `original-plan`: The original plan, goals, or expected outcomes for the event

## Outputs

- `aar-scope`: Defined scope: what time period, what activities, and what outcomes are being reviewed
- `factual-timeline`: Factual timeline of what actually happened, assembled from records, logs, and participant accounts

## Process

1. Define the scope: what specific event, project phase, or incident is being reviewed? Narrow scope produces better results than broad scope. Review one sprint, not the whole project.
2. Assemble the factual timeline from objective sources: logs, emails, chat messages, metrics, and timelines. Get the facts straight before opinions enter the room.
3. Identify the participants: everyone who was involved in the event being reviewed. The AAR requires people who were there, not managers who were watching from afar.
4. Send the scope and factual timeline to participants 24 hours before the review so they can prepare their observations.
