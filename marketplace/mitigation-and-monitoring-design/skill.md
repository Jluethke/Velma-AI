# Mitigation And Monitoring Design

Design mitigation measures to reduce significant impacts and a monitoring program to verify effectiveness.

## Inputs

- `impact-assessment`: Predicted impacts with significance ratings
- `baseline-conditions`: Baseline for monitoring comparison

## Outputs

- `mitigation-measures`: Specific mitigation for each significant impact, following the hierarchy: avoid, minimize, restore, offset
- `monitoring-program`: Monitoring parameters, locations, frequency, trigger levels, and contingency actions

## Process

1. Apply mitigation hierarchy. Avoid: modify project design to eliminate the impact (realign route to avoid habitat, relocate noisy operations away from receptors). Minimize: reduce the magnitude (noise barriers, dust suppression, silt fences, working hours restrictions). Restore: return affected area to pre-project condition (habitat reinstatement, landscaping). Offset: compensate for residual impacts elsewhere (biodiversity offsetting, carbon credits). Avoid is always preferred; offset is last resort.
2. Noise mitigation. Construction: best practical means (BPM): low-noise equipment, acoustic enclosures, barriers, restricted working hours (typically 07:00-19:00 weekdays, 07:00-13:00 Saturday, no Sunday/holiday), section 61 consent (UK) or noise variance (US). Operational: noise barriers (insertion loss from Maekawa formula: IL = 10*log(3+20*N) dB, where N is Fresnel number), low-noise road surface (3 to 5 dB reduction), building insulation for residual exceedances.
3. Ecology mitigation. Timing restrictions (avoid bird nesting season March-August, bat hibernation November-March for northern hemisphere). Translocation of protected species (great crested newts, reptiles) to receptor sites prepared in advance. Habitat creation: like-for-like or better replacement at specified ratio (2:1 or 3:1 for high-value habitat). Wildlife crossings: underpasses, overpasses, ledges in culverts for otters. Long-term management plan for created habitats (minimum 30 years).
4. Water mitigation. Construction: settlement ponds for site runoff, spill containment for fuel and chemical storage, wheel wash to prevent mud on roads. Operational: sustainable drainage systems (SuDS): swales, detention basins, permeable paving, constructed wetlands. Design to treat and attenuate runoff to greenfield rates for all events up to 1-in-100-year plus climate change allowance.
5. Design monitoring program. Pre-construction: confirm baseline and establish reference conditions. During construction: noise monitoring at nearest receptors (continuous where predicted to exceed thresholds), dust monitoring (PM10 or visual), water quality at discharge points, ecological watching brief during site clearance. Post-construction: habitat establishment monitoring (annual for 5 years minimum), noise compliance monitoring, water quality. Define trigger levels and contingency actions: if trigger exceeded, implement additional mitigation within defined response time.
