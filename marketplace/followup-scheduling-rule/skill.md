# Followup Scheduling Rule

Define how every follow-up gets a specific date rather than living on an undated someday list, because an undated follow-up is not scheduled, it is merely hoped for, and the specific date is what moves it from intention to commitment.

## Inputs

- `action-extraction-rules`: Rules for extracting one concrete follow-up action
- `action-quality-criteria`: The test for whether an action is concrete enough

## Outputs

- `scheduling-rules`: Rules for assigning every follow-up a specific do-date, including how to time follow-ups appropriately
- `due-date-logic`: How to set the date based on urgency, the promise made, and the natural rhythm of the relationship

## Process

1. Require a specific date on every follow-up. Someday is not a date, and a task without a date drifts indefinitely. Even a rough but specific date (next Tuesday) is a commitment where someday is not.
2. Time the follow-up appropriately rather than always immediately. Some follow-ups are urgent (a promise to send something today), some are better paced (a check-in that would feel pushy the next day but natural in two weeks). The date reflects the right timing, not just the soonest.
3. Honor any timing you committed to. If you told someone you would follow up after their launch, the date is after their launch, not whenever you remember. The specific date is how you keep the implicit promise.
4. Put the dated follow-up where it will surface on its day: a task manager with due dates, a calendar, or a tickler system. A date that does not resurface the task when it comes due is just a note.
