# Current Movement Baseline

Honestly assess your current movement level: how much do you move, what do you enjoy, and what has failed before.

## Inputs

- `current-activity`: How much you currently move: exercise, walking, stairs, active hobbies
- `past-attempts`: Exercise programs you have tried and abandoned, and why they failed

## Outputs

- `movement-baseline`: Current weekly movement: hours, types, and frequency
- `failure-patterns`: Why past exercise attempts failed: too ambitious, too boring, too time-consuming, injury, schedule conflict

## Process

1. Be honest about the baseline. If you walk 10 minutes a day and nothing else, that is the baseline. Overstating the baseline leads to an overambitious plan that fails.
2. Review past failures. The most common reasons exercise programs fail: started too aggressively (went from zero to 5 days/week), chose an activity you do not enjoy, required too much time, or required special equipment or location.
3. Identify what you do enjoy. Walking, swimming, cycling, dancing, sports, yoga. If the activity is not enjoyable, the habit will not survive. Discipline is a finite resource; enjoyment is not.
4. The goal is not fitness. The goal is a movement habit that you will still be doing in 5 years. This changes the design criteria entirely.
