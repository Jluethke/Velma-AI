# Cccm Adjustment Planner

For each key gap, plan the specific adjustment to the listener's expectations and note how not to misread them. Adaptation runs both ways: you change how you send, and you reinterpret how you receive. A direct message from an indirect culture carries more weight than its plain words show.

## Inputs

- `key-gaps`: The dimensions where the gap matters most
- `gap-risk-ranking`: The gaps ranked by potential damage

## Outputs

- `adjustment-actions`: For each key gap, the specific change in how you send your message
- `do-not-misread-notes`: For each key gap, how to correctly interpret what the counterpart sends given their position

## Process

1. For each gap, write the specific adjustment to how you communicate. Facing an indirect-feedback culture, soften criticism and read a mild word as a strong signal.
2. Write the matching interpretation note. A yes from a confrontation-avoiding, high-context counterpart may mean 'I hear you,' not 'I agree.'
3. Adjust toward the listener's expectations, since across cultures the burden of adapting falls on the communicator who wants to be understood.
4. Keep adjustments to the top gaps so your communication stays natural. Overcorrecting on every dimension reads as awkward and can give offense of its own.
