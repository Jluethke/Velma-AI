# Plateau Diagnosis

Confirm you are actually on a plateau (not just in a normal dip) and identify when the plateau started.

## Inputs

- `skill-description`: The skill where you feel stuck
- `performance-history`: Evidence of your performance over time (scores, output quality, speed, feedback)

## Outputs

- `plateau-confirmation`: Whether this is a true plateau (no measurable improvement for 4+ weeks despite regular practice) or a normal fluctuation
- `plateau-onset`: When the plateau started and what your practice looked like just before it started

## Process

1. Plot your performance over time using whatever metric you have. A plateau is a flat line for 4 or more weeks. A dip is a temporary decline followed by improvement. If you have fewer than 4 weeks of flat performance, you may not be on a plateau.
2. Check that you have actually been practicing during the flat period. No practice means no plateau; it means absence.
3. Identify the date the plateau started. What were you doing just before? Often a plateau starts right after a period of rapid improvement, because you reached the limit of your current practice method.
4. Rate your practice quality during the plateau: are you still doing focused, effortful practice, or has practice become comfortable repetition? Comfortable repetition is the most common cause of plateaus.
