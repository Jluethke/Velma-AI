# Trust Ledger Relationship Selection

Select the top 20 relationships to put on the ledger and tag each by context, because tracking every relationship is unsustainable and the value comes from attention to the relationships where a drained balance would cost you the most.

## Inputs

- `relationship-pool`: The full set of relationships you could track across work and personal life
- `relationship-context`: The contexts in play: personal, manager-and-report, client, vendor, or a mix

## Outputs

- `top-relationships`: The 20 relationships that go on the ledger, chosen by importance and by the cost of a drained balance
- `context-tags`: Each relationship tagged by context, since deposits and withdrawals mean different things in each

## Process

1. List candidate relationships and score each on two axes: how much you depend on it, and how damaging a low balance would be. A vendor you rarely need but who holds a single point of failure can still make the list.
2. Take the top 20. Fewer is fine. More than 20 and the ledger becomes a chore you abandon. The point is sustained attention to the few that carry real weight.
3. Tag each with its context (personal, manager, report, client, vendor). The context determines what counts as a deposit later, so do not skip it.
4. Note any relationship that spans two contexts (a colleague who is also a friend). These carry two ledgers that can diverge, and a withdrawal in one context can draw down the balance in the other.
