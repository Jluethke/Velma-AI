# Referral Motivation Mapper

Map the motivations that drive people to make referrals across different relationship types, so you can design a system that activates the right motivation for each referrer.

## Inputs

- `referral-context`: Whether this is for personal networking, B2B sales, B2C growth, or professional services
- `referrer-profiles`: The types of people who refer (clients, friends, colleagues, partners) and what they typically value

## Outputs

- `motivation-map`: For each referrer type, the primary and secondary motivations: social capital, reciprocity, financial incentive, status, altruism, or obligation
- `anti-motivations`: What discourages referrals: reputation risk, inconvenience, unclear value to the referred party, feeling used

## Process

1. Identify the 6 referral motivations. Social capital: referring makes the referrer look connected and knowledgeable. Reciprocity: they owe you a favor or want to bank one. Financial incentive: they receive a direct reward. Status: referring elevates their standing in a community. Altruism: they genuinely think the match will help both parties. Obligation: they feel social pressure to refer (weakest and least sustainable).
2. Map each referrer type to their primary motivation. Clients primarily refer for social capital (they want their peers to have a good experience and to look smart for recommending). Friends refer for reciprocity and altruism. Partners refer for financial incentive and status. Colleagues refer for social capital and obligation. Getting the motivation wrong wastes the ask.
3. Map the anti-motivations. The biggest referral killer is reputation risk: if the referral goes badly, the referrer looks bad. Second is inconvenience: if making the referral requires effort (filling out a form, writing an email), many people will not bother. Third is unclear value: if the referrer cannot articulate why their contact should care, they will not make the introduction.
4. Design the referral ask to activate the primary motivation and neutralize the anti-motivations. For social-capital-motivated referrers: make them look good by giving them a specific success story they can share. For convenience: provide a copy-paste introduction they can send in 30 seconds. For reputation risk: offer a no-commitment first step (a free consultation, a casual coffee) so the referrer is not staking their credibility on a sale.
