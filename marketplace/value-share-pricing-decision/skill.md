# Value Share Pricing Decision

Set the price as a fraction of the value delivered, choosing the fraction based on competitive alternatives and customer power.

## Inputs

- `annual-value-per-customer`: The value ceiling
- `value-confidence`: Confidence in the value estimate

## Outputs

- `price-as-value-share`: The price stated as a percentage of value delivered (typical range: 10-30% of value)
- `pricing-rationale`: Why this specific share was chosen (competitive landscape, customer alternatives, switching costs)

## Process

1. The standard value-share range is 10-30% of the value delivered. At 10%, the customer keeps 90% of the value and has a strong incentive to buy. At 30%, the deal is less obvious but still favorable for the customer.
2. Choose the percentage based on: competitive alternatives (if competitors offer similar value, you need to share more with the customer), switching costs (if switching to you is easy, charge less; if hard, you can charge more), and the customer's sophistication (sophisticated buyers will calculate the value themselves).
3. State the price in terms of value: 'This costs $12,000 per year and saves you approximately $50,000 per year. You keep 76% of the value.' This framing makes the price feel like an investment, not a cost.
4. If the value share exceeds 30%, reconsider. At that point, the customer's ROI may not be strong enough to overcome inertia and risk.
