# Escalation Move Designer

Design the specific moves that advance trust from one level to the next, including the reciprocity test at each level.

## Inputs

- `trust-levels`: The defined trust levels with behavioral markers
- `evidence-checklist`: Observable behaviors confirming each level

## Outputs

- `escalation-moves`: For each level transition, the specific actions to take that signal you are willing to move to the next level
- `reciprocity-tests`: How to verify that the other party reciprocates at each level before escalating further

## Process

1. Design escalation moves for each transition. 0 to 1: share a useful insight relevant to their specific situation (not a broadcast), ask a genuine question about their work. 1 to 2: introduce them to a specific person in your network (not a mass introduction), endorse them publicly in a small setting. 2 to 3: share a non-public plan or challenge, ask for their input on a sensitive decision. 3 to 4: propose a joint commitment with shared risk (co-invest, co-create, formally partner).
2. Build the reciprocity test into each move. After making an escalation move, wait. Do not make a second move at the same level until they reciprocate. If you share a useful insight (level 1 move) and they reciprocate with their own insight, you are both at level 1. If they do not reciprocate after two attempts, they are not at that level regardless of how they respond conversationally.
3. Define the timing of the reciprocity window. Level 1 reciprocity should come within 2-4 weeks. Level 2 within 1-3 months. Level 3 within 3-6 months. Level 4 reciprocity has no fixed window but should come within the context of the commitment. Faster reciprocity generally indicates higher trust velocity.
4. Set the escalation pace: never skip a level. The temptation in both personal and business relationships is to jump from level 1 to level 3 when you feel a strong connection. But skipping level 2 means you have not tested reputational trust. If they will not introduce you to their network, they should not be trusted with your strategic information.
