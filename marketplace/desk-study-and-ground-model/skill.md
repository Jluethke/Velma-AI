# Desk Study And Ground Model

Compile existing information and develop preliminary ground model before field investigation.

## Inputs

- `site-location`: Project site location and extent
- `project-requirements`: Type of structure, loads, and construction method driving investigation needs

## Outputs

- `preliminary-ground-model`: Expected stratigraphy, hazards, and key geotechnical questions to answer
- `investigation-plan`: Borehole locations, depths, in-situ and lab testing program

## Process

1. Gather published geological maps (USGS/BGS), soil survey maps, previous site investigation reports from nearby projects, aerial photographs (historical for fill/excavation detection), and utility records.
2. Identify geological hazards: faults, landslide susceptibility, liquefaction potential (in seismic zones with loose saturated sand), expansive soils, karst (sinkholes in limestone), mine workings, contamination.
3. Develop preliminary ground model: expected soil/rock types, approximate layer thicknesses, likely groundwater regime. Identify key unknowns that the investigation must resolve.
4. Plan investigation. Borehole spacing: 15 to 40 m for buildings, 25 to 100 m for linear infrastructure. Depth: 1.5 times foundation width below founding level for shallow foundations, or to competent rock for piled foundations. Include at least one deep borehole to full depth of influence.
5. Select in-situ testing program by anticipated ground type and design needs. CPTu everywhere possible (continuous profiling). SPT in boreholes where CPT refusal occurs. Vane shear in soft clay. Pressuremeter in rock or stiff soil. Flat plate dilatometer for settlement prediction in sand.
