# Repair What Repair Looks Like

Ask the other person what repair would actually look like to them, rather than assuming, because people need different things to rebuild (some need acknowledgment, some need changed behavior, some need time, some need a specific amends) and imposing your idea of repair often misses what they actually require.

## Inputs

- `ownership-statement`: The delivered statement of responsibility
- `non-equivocation-check`: The confirmation the statement held no hidden defense

## Outputs

- `repair-definition-questions`: The questions that invite the other person to define what rebuilding trust would require from you
- `listening-protocol`: How to receive their answer: without defending, bargaining, or correcting, even when it is hard to hear

## Process

1. Ask directly and openly what rebuilding would require. Questions like what would help, what do you need from me, and what would it take to trust me again hand them the definition of repair instead of you assuming it.
2. Be ready for a range of answers. Some people need only to feel heard, some need a concrete change, some need an amends, some need time and space, and some are not ready to answer yet. Each is legitimate and tells you what the repair actually is.
3. Set the listening protocol before you ask, because their answer may be hard to hear. The discipline is to receive it without defending, correcting their account, or negotiating the terms down. You asked, so you listen.
4. Accept that they may not know yet, or may need the question to sit. Pushing for an immediate answer serves your urgency, not their repair, so leave room for the question to be answered over time.
