# Dcf Three Conversations Separator

Every hard conversation is three tangled conversations at once: what happened, the feelings involved, and the identity at stake. Pull them apart before you plan anything, because a sentence that mixes all three lands as an attack.

## Inputs

- `conversation-situation`: What the conversation is about and what you have been avoiding saying
- `other-party-context`: Who the other person is, your relationship, and what you know about their view

## Outputs

- `what-happened-conversation`: The facts, the differing stories about them, and where you each assign blame
- `feelings-conversation`: The emotions present for you and likely present for them, named rather than acted out
- `identity-conversation`: What this situation says to each of you about who you are, the quiet threat underneath

## Process

1. Write the situation out once in full, then tag each sentence as a fact claim, a feeling, or an identity statement. Most sentences will be a blend. Split them.
2. For the what-happened strand, list the objective facts separately from each side's story about those facts. Two people can agree on the facts and still tell opposite stories.
3. For the feelings strand, name the emotions on both sides. Do not decide yet whether they are justified. Naming is not agreeing.
4. For the identity strand, finish the sentence 'If this is true, it means I am ...' for yourself and guess the same for them. That ending is the real stake.
