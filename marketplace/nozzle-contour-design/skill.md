# Nozzle Contour Design

Design the nozzle contour for maximum thrust coefficient at the design altitude, balancing performance against length and mass.

## Inputs

- `throat-area`: Nozzle throat area from chamber sizing
- `chamber-pressure-target-pa`: Chamber pressure
- `theoretical-performance`: Gas properties from thermochemistry (gamma, molecular weight, temperature)

## Outputs

- `expansion-ratio`: Exit area / throat area selected for design altitude
- `nozzle-contour`: Wall contour coordinates from throat to exit
- `nozzle-length`: Overall nozzle length and mass estimate
- `performance-at-altitude`: Thrust and Isp at sea level, max-Q altitude, and vacuum

## Process

1. Select expansion ratio from design altitude: for a sea-level engine, expand to Pe = Pambient at the design point (but over-expanded at sea level is acceptable if Pe > 0.4*Pambient to avoid flow separation). For vacuum engines, expansion ratio is limited by nozzle mass and length.
2. The Rao optimum contour maximizes thrust for a given nozzle length. It is shorter than a 15-degree conical nozzle at the same expansion ratio and delivers 1-2% more performance. The contour is computed by method of characteristics with a variational optimization.
3. For practical design, the truncated ideal contour (TIC) or thrust-optimized contour (TOC) is generated by specifying the nozzle length as a fraction of a 15-degree cone (typically 80%). The initial expansion angle after the throat is 20-35 degrees, with a parabolic turn to the exit angle of 8-15 degrees.
4. Altitude compensation: a fixed nozzle is a compromise. At sea level, the flow is over-expanded (exit pressure below ambient) and a shock forms inside the nozzle (flow separation if too far over-expanded). At vacuum, the flow is under-expanded (wasted energy in radial momentum). Altitude-compensating nozzles (aerospike, extendible nozzle) partially solve this.
5. Calculate thrust vs altitude: F = mdot * Ve + (Pe - Pa) * Ae. At sea level, the pressure term is negative for an over-expanded nozzle. At vacuum, Pa = 0 and the full exit area contributes. The Isp difference between sea level and vacuum can be 30-60 seconds.
