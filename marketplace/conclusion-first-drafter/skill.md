# Conclusion First Drafter

Write the conclusion first. This is the single sentence that answers the reader's question or states the recommendation.

## Inputs

- `communication-purpose`: What this communication needs to accomplish (inform, persuade, request, decide)
- `audience`: Who is reading and what they care about
- `key-information`: The information, analysis, or recommendation to communicate

## Outputs

- `conclusion-statement`: The single sentence that the entire communication supports. If the reader reads only this sentence, they know the answer.
- `audience-question`: The question in the reader's mind that this communication answers

## Process

1. Identify the question in the reader's mind. A status update answers 'are we on track?' A proposal answers 'should we do this?' A report answers 'what happened and what does it mean?'
2. Write the answer to that question in one sentence. This is the conclusion. It goes first, not last.
3. Test the conclusion: if the reader reads only this sentence and nothing else, do they know the answer? If not, sharpen it.
4. The conclusion is not a summary of the communication. It is the answer. 'This report covers three areas' is a summary. 'We should expand to the European market in Q3' is a conclusion.
