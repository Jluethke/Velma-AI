# Source Material Preparation

Prepare the source material for progressive summarization. Ensure the full original is preserved and accessible. Add the capture metadata: source, date, and the reason you captured it.

## Inputs

- `source-text`: The original text to summarize progressively (article, notes, transcript, document)
- `capture-reason`: Why this material was captured (the project or question it relates to)

## Outputs

- `prepared-source`: Source material with metadata (origin, date, capture reason) preserved in its original form
- `initial-read-notes`: Brief notes from the first reading: what stood out, what surprised, what connects to current work

## Process

1. Preserve the full original text. Never edit, rewrite, or reorganize the original. It is the bottom layer that all other layers compress from.
2. Add capture metadata: the source URL or citation, the date captured, and one sentence about why you captured it.
3. On the first read-through, note what stands out. Do not try to be systematic. Resonance is the signal: passages that make you stop, re-read, or think 'I need to remember this' are the ones to note.
4. If the material is longer than 2000 words, note the sections that seem most relevant to your capture reason. You will focus your first bolding pass on those sections.
