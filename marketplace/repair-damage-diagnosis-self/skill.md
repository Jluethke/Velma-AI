# Repair Damage Diagnosis Self

Diagnose the damage honestly from your own perspective first: what happened, what you did, and what the real harm was, because a repair built on a vague or self-serving account of the damage will address the wrong thing, and you cannot own a part you have not clearly named.

## Inputs

- `relationship-background`: The history and nature of the relationship and why it matters enough to repair
- `incident-summary`: What happened, as best you can reconstruct it, including the events that led to the damage

## Outputs

- `self-perspective-diagnosis`: Your honest account of what happened and what the real damage to the relationship was
- `self-contribution-notes`: The specific things you did or failed to do that contributed, named plainly without justification

## Process

1. Reconstruct what happened as factually as you can, separating the events from your interpretation of them. The goal is an account you could state plainly, not a case for your own defense.
2. Name the real damage, which is usually about trust, respect, or safety rather than the surface incident. The argument was about a missed deadline, but the damage was the other person learning they could not rely on you.
3. List your specific contributions plainly: the things you did, the things you failed to do, the patterns you brought. Write them without the word but, because every justification you attach here is one you will leak later and undermine the repair.
4. Resist the pull to make yourself the misunderstood party. A diagnosis that conveniently concludes you were mostly right is not a diagnosis, it is a defense, and it guarantees the repair will fail at the ownership step.
