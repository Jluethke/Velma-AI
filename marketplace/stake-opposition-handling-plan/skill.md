# Stake Opposition Handling Plan

Build the plan to address the opposed, now that the neutrals have shifted the balance, choosing deliberately for each opponent among genuine conversion, negotiated accommodation, containment, or routing around them, since not every opponent can or needs to be won over.

## Inputs

- `neutral-conversion-plan`: The plan and sequence for converting neutrals
- `conversion-sequence`: The order of approach that builds momentum

## Outputs

- `opposition-handling-plan`: For each opponent, the chosen approach: convert via their lever, negotiate an accommodation, contain their influence, or route around them
- `escalation-guardrails`: The limits on how far to push, so handling opposition does not create lasting enemies or a damaging fight

## Process

1. Address the opposed only after the neutrals have moved, because an opponent isolated from the fence-sitters they used to anchor is far more tractable than one leading a faction.
2. Choose an approach per opponent rather than defaulting to persuasion. Convert the ones whose interest you can genuinely serve, negotiate an accommodation with those who need something specific, contain the influence of those you cannot move, and simply route around the ones whose opposition does not actually block the outcome.
3. Know when not to fight. Energy spent arguing with an implacable low-influence opponent is energy wasted, and some opposition is best left alone once it can no longer sway the decision.
4. Set guardrails on escalation. Winning the decision while making a lasting enemy of someone you will work with again is often a net loss, so decide in advance how hard to push and preserve the relationship where you can, because today's opponent is tomorrow's stakeholder on another project.
