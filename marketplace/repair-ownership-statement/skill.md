# Repair Ownership Statement

Compose and deliver an ownership statement that takes responsibility for your part without equivocation, because the equivocating apology (I am sorry if you were hurt, I am sorry but you also) is the single most common point where repair attempts fail, since it signals you are still defending rather than owning.

## Inputs

- `other-perspective-diagnosis`: How the events looked and felt from their side
- `perspective-gap-map`: Where your account and theirs diverge

## Outputs

- `ownership-statement`: A clear statement taking responsibility for your specific part, naming the harm, without excuse or counter-accusation
- `non-equivocation-check`: The check that the statement contains no hidden defense: no if, no but, no shifting blame, no demand for immediate forgiveness

## Process

1. Build the statement from your contribution notes and their perspective: name specifically what you did, acknowledge the harm it caused them in their terms, and express genuine regret for it.
2. Strip out every equivocation. I am sorry if you felt that way is not ownership, it is a suggestion they overreacted. I am sorry but you also is a counter-attack wearing an apology. Cut the if, the but, and the and-you-too entirely.
3. Own only your part, and own it fully rather than partially. You are not responsible for their contribution and should not claim it, but the part that is yours gets owned without hedging, because partial ownership reads as negotiation.
4. Make no demand for forgiveness or immediate repair in return. The ownership stands on its own, and attaching so now we are good to it converts the apology into a transaction and tells them you want the discomfort over more than you want to repair the harm.
