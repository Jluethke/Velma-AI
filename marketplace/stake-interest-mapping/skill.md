# Stake Interest Mapping

Map what each stakeholder actually cares about, separating their stated position from the underlying interest driving it, because people take positions for reasons, and you move a position only by addressing the interest beneath it, not by arguing against the position itself.

## Inputs

- `position-map`: Each stakeholder's position toward the outcome
- `position-confidence`: Confidence in each position read

## Outputs

- `interest-map`: For each stakeholder, the underlying interests driving their position: what they protect, want, fear, or are measured on
- `stated-vs-real-interests`: Where a stakeholder's stated reason differs from the real interest, so you address the right thing

## Process

1. For each stakeholder, dig past the position to the interest. Someone opposing a change may really be protecting their team's workload, their status, a metric they are judged on, or simply their sense of control. The position is the symptom.
2. Distinguish stated reasons from real interests. The stated objection (this is technically risky) often masks the real one (this threatens my role). Addressing the stated reason while missing the real one changes nothing.
3. Identify what each stakeholder is measured and rewarded on, because self-interest around incentives drives more positions than people admit. A stakeholder whose bonus depends on a metric your project threatens will oppose it regardless of its merits.
4. Note the interests you can genuinely serve versus those you cannot. Some opposition rests on interests you can accommodate, and some on interests fundamentally at odds with the outcome, and knowing which is which shapes whether to convert or to route around them.
