# Cccm Counterpart Position Plotter

Plot your counterpart's likely position on each dimension, using evidence and holding the estimate loosely. Culture predicts tendencies, not individuals, so a national pattern is a starting hypothesis to be corrected by what the actual person does. The aim is a working estimate, not a stereotype.

## Inputs

- `dimension-definitions`: The eight dimensions as spectrums
- `setting-summary`: The interaction and the dimensions most likely to matter

## Outputs

- `counterpart-positions`: The counterpart's estimated position on each dimension that matters for this interaction
- `evidence-caveats`: Where the estimate is weak and where the individual may differ from the cultural pattern

## Process

1. Estimate the counterpart's position on each relevant dimension from cultural patterns and from anything you have observed directly.
2. Weight observed behavior over general pattern. An individual who has worked across cultures may sit far from their national default.
3. Mark the estimates you are least sure of, so you watch for evidence that corrects them early in the interaction.
4. Hold the estimate as a hypothesis. Treating a cultural tendency as a certain fact about a person is the error the map is meant to prevent.
