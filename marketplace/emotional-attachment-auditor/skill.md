# Emotional Attachment Auditor

For the hardest cases, audit the emotional attachment. Some commitments resist the fresh-start test not because of financial sunk costs but because of identity attachment: 'I am the kind of person who finishes what they start' or 'I have been doing this for 10 years; quitting means those years were wasted.' Name the emotional driver. Naming it reduces its power over the decision.

## Inputs

- `sunk-cost-suspects`: Commitments that failed the fresh-start test
- `switching-cost-analysis`: The switching cost calculations

## Outputs

- `emotional-drivers`: For each suspect commitment: the emotional reason you are holding on, named explicitly
- `reframed-narratives`: Alternative narratives that make stopping feel acceptable (e.g., 'quitting this frees me to do X' instead of 'I wasted 5 years')

## Process

1. For each commitment you know you should stop but feel unable to, ask: what story am I telling myself about why I must continue?
2. Common stories: 'I invested too much to quit now', 'quitting is for people who cannot handle it', 'what will people think', 'those years would be wasted'.
3. Name the story explicitly. Write it down.
4. Write an alternative story that makes stopping feel like a decision, not a failure. 'I am redirecting resources from something that is not working to something that can.'
