# Aar Four Questions

Run the four-question AAR discussion. Each question builds on the last. The discipline is to stay on one question until it is answered before moving to the next.

## Inputs

- `aar-scope`: Defined scope of the review
- `factual-timeline`: Factual timeline of events

## Outputs

- `aar-discussion-record`: Record of the four-question discussion: plan, reality, gap analysis, and proposed actions
- `gap-analysis`: Specific gaps between plan and reality with identified root causes

## Process

1. Question 1: What was supposed to happen? State the original plan, goals, and expected outcomes. Get agreement on what success looked like before the event. This is factual, not controversial.
2. Question 2: What actually happened? Walk through the factual timeline. Fill in gaps from participant accounts. The rule: no blame, no judgment, only facts. If someone says 'Bob dropped the ball,' redirect to 'What happened with the handoff to Bob?'
3. Question 3: Why was there a difference? This is the analysis. For each gap between plan and reality, ask why until you reach a root cause. Surface-level answers ('we ran out of time') need follow-up ('why did we run out of time? what took longer than expected? why did we underestimate?').
4. Question 4: What will we do differently next time? This is the output. Every answer must be a specific action, not a principle. 'Communicate better' is not an action. 'Send a status update to the channel every Friday by 4pm' is an action.
