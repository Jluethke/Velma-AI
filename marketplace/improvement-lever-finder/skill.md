# Improvement Lever Finder

Identify which specific 1% improvements have the highest absolute impact, because not all 1% improvements are equal.

## Inputs

- `metric-to-model`: The metric being modeled
- `projection-table`: The three scenario projections

## Outputs

- `lever-ranking`: The specific 1% improvements available, ranked by their absolute dollar or unit impact over 10 years
- `highest-impact-lever`: The single 1% improvement that produces the largest absolute gain, with the specific action required to achieve it

## Process

1. List the specific ways to achieve a 1% improvement in this metric. For savings: earn 1% more, spend 1% less, or get 1% better returns. For revenue: increase price 1%, increase volume 1%, or reduce churn 1%. For weight loss: eat 1% fewer calories or burn 1% more.
2. For each lever, calculate the 10-year absolute impact. A 1% price increase on a $100 product across 1,000 units per month produces $120,000 over 10 years. A 1% volume increase on the same product produces $120,000 over 10 years. But a 1% churn reduction on a subscription business compounds differently because retained customers produce revenue every month.
3. Rank the levers by absolute 10-year impact. The top lever is the one to focus on. Trying to improve all levers simultaneously dilutes effort. Improving the top lever by 5% beats improving all levers by 1%.
4. For the top lever, write the specific action: what exactly would you do differently starting tomorrow to achieve this 1% improvement? If the action is vague, decompose it until it is concrete.
