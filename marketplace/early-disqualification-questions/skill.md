# Early Disqualification Questions

Design questions that surface disqualification criteria in the first conversation, without being abrasive.

## Inputs

- `disqualification-criteria`: What makes a bad fit
- `product-fit`: What your product does

## Outputs

- `disqualification-questions`: 5-7 questions that each map to a disqualification criterion, phrased to sound helpful rather than interrogative
- `question-timing`: When in the first conversation to ask each question (early, but after rapport is established)

## Process

1. For each criterion, write a question that surfaces it naturally. Instead of 'Do you have budget?' ask 'What is your process for evaluating and approving new investments in this area?' The answer reveals whether budget exists without making it a yes/no gate.
2. Phrase questions as helpful: 'I want to make sure I do not waste your time. A few quick questions to see if this is a fit.' Most prospects appreciate efficiency.
3. Ask the most likely disqualification question first. If the most common reason deals fail is 'wrong company size,' ask about company size early.
4. Listen for both the explicit answer and the implicit signal. 'We do not really have a budget for this' is explicit. 'That is an interesting idea, we might look at that next year' is an implicit 'no budget now.'
