# Decision Framing

Frame the single decision this meeting must make. If the meeting has no decision, cancel it.

## Inputs

- `meeting-topic`: What the meeting is about
- `context`: Background: why does this decision need to be made now

## Outputs

- `decision-statement`: The single decision in the form: 'We need to decide [specific choice] by [deadline] because [consequence of not deciding]'
- `decision-options`: The 2-4 options to choose between, each with pros/cons pre-documented

## Process

1. State the decision in one sentence: 'We need to decide whether to launch in Q1 or Q2.' If you cannot state the decision in one sentence, the meeting is not about a decision.
2. If the meeting has no decision (it is a status update, a brainstorm, or an FYI), cancel it. Status updates are emails. Brainstorms are documents. FYIs are Slack messages.
3. Document 2-4 options with pros and cons before the meeting. The meeting is for choosing between prepared options, not for discovering options in real time.
4. Every meeting must have a deadline consequence: 'We need to decide by Friday because engineering starts the sprint Monday.' Without urgency, decisions drift.
