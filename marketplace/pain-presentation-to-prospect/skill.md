# Pain Presentation To Prospect

Present the quantified pain to the prospect in a way that frames your price as an investment against a larger cost.

## Inputs

- `pain-in-dollars`: Each pain point in dollars
- `total-annual-pain`: Total annual cost of the pain

## Outputs

- `pain-presentation`: A one-page summary showing: the pain in the prospect's words, the dollar amount, and the formula used
- `pricing-frame`: Your price presented as a percentage of the pain eliminated: 'This costs $X/year and addresses a $Y/year problem'

## Process

1. Present the one-page summary: 'Based on what you told me, this problem costs your organization approximately $X per year. Here is how I calculated that. [Show the formula with their numbers.]'
2. Let the prospect react. They may adjust the numbers up or down. Either is fine. The goal is not a precise number; the goal is a credible order of magnitude that puts your price in context.
3. Frame your price against the pain: 'Our product costs $12,000 per year. Based on your numbers, it addresses approximately $60,000 in annual costs. The ROI is approximately 5:1.'
4. Do not proceed to pricing until the prospect has agreed that the pain number is roughly correct. If they dispute it, recalculate with their corrections. The pain number must be their number, not yours.
