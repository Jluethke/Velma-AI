# Decision Log Template Design

Design the decision log template with six required fields. The reasoning field has the highest bar: it must be specific enough that a new person can understand why this option was chosen over the alternatives.

## Inputs

- `decision-context`: The scope of the decision log: personal, team, organization, or project
- `decision-frequency`: How often decisions are made that should be logged (daily, weekly, monthly)

## Outputs

- `decision-record-template`: Template with six fields: question, options, choice, reasoning, expected outcome, review date
- `reasoning-quality-standard`: Examples of good and bad reasoning entries to calibrate the quality bar

## Process

1. The six fields: (1) Question: what are we deciding, framed as a question. (2) Options: the alternatives considered, including 'do nothing.' (3) Choice: which option was selected. (4) Reasoning: why this option and not the others. (5) Expected outcome: what we expect to happen as a result. (6) Review date: when we should check if the decision was right.
2. The reasoning field standard: it must reference specific facts, constraints, or tradeoffs. 'It felt right' is not reasoning. 'Option B required 3 additional hires we do not have budget for, and Option C depended on a vendor integration that has missed two deadlines' is reasoning.
3. Provide three examples of good decision records and three examples of bad ones (vague reasoning, missing options, no review date) to calibrate the quality bar.
4. Keep the template simple. If logging a decision takes more than 10 minutes, the template is too complex and people will skip it.
