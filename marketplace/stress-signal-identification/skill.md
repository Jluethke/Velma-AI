# Stress Signal Identification

Identify your personal stress signals: the physical and behavioral signs that appear before you consciously feel stressed.

## Inputs

- `stress-history`: Times when you have been highly stressed: what triggered it, how it felt, what you did
- `current-stress-level`: Your current stress level and what is driving it

## Outputs

- `physical-signals`: Your body's stress signals: jaw clenching, shoulder tension, shallow breathing, stomach tightness, headache, racing heart
- `behavioral-signals`: Your behavioral stress signals: snapping at people, checking phone compulsively, eating for comfort, procrastinating, difficulty sleeping

## Process

1. Think about the last 3 times you were highly stressed. Before you consciously realized you were stressed, what was your body doing? Jaw clenched? Shoulders up? Breathing shallow?
2. Identify your behavioral tells. Stress shows in behavior before it shows in conscious awareness. Some people eat. Some withdraw. Some get irritable. Some clean obsessively. Know your pattern.
3. Create a short checklist of your top 3 physical and top 3 behavioral signals. Check this list twice daily (morning and afternoon) to catch stress early.
4. Early detection is the key: a cortisol spike that is interrupted in the first 90 seconds dissipates quickly. A cortisol spike that runs for hours becomes chronic stress.
