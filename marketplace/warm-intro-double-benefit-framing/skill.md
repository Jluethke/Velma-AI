# Warm Intro Double Benefit Framing

Frame the introduction as a benefit to both the target and the connector, not just to you, and build in the reputation protection that makes a connector comfortable, since the biggest reason introductions do not happen is the connector's fear of looking bad if it goes poorly.

## Inputs

- `forwardable-blurb`: The self-contained blurb to forward
- `blurb-variants`: Versions tuned to ask type and target priorities

## Outputs

- `mutual-benefit-statement`: A clear statement of what the target gains and what the connector gains from making the introduction
- `referrer-risk-reducers`: The elements that lower the connector's reputation risk: a low-commitment first step, an easy out, and no pressure on the target

## Process

1. Name what the target gains. If you cannot articulate why the introduction is good for them independent of what you want, the introduction is a favor, not a match, and it will convert poorly and strain the connector.
2. Name what the connector gains. Often it is social capital (they look connected and helpful) or the satisfaction of a good match. Make it easy for them to feel good about forwarding it.
3. Reduce the connector's risk directly. Offer the target a low-commitment first step (a short call, no obligation) and an explicit easy out, so that if the target is not interested, the connector has not spent credibility pushing something unwanted.
4. Remove pressure from the ask. The connector should be able to forward the blurb and step back, not feel they must advocate or chase. Low-pressure introductions preserve the connector's willingness to introduce you again.
