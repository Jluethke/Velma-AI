# Expertise Extraction Method

Extract expertise from knowledge holders using the critical incident technique. Ask them to describe non-obvious judgment calls, not routine procedures. Document the cues they noticed, the options they considered, and what a less experienced person would have missed.

## Inputs

- `decision-log-template`: Template for capturing decisions
- `decision-capture-trigger-list`: Triggers for decision logging

## Outputs

- `expert-interview-protocol`: Interview protocol for extracting tacit knowledge using critical incident technique
- `case-study-template`: Template for documenting expertise as case studies (situation, cues, judgment, outcome, lesson)

## Process

1. Identify the knowledge holders: the people others go to with questions. These are not always the most senior people. They are the people who get interrupted most often because they know things others do not.
2. Use the critical incident technique: ask 'Tell me about a time when you had to make a non-obvious judgment call in your work.' Follow up: 'What cues told you something was off? What options did you consider? What would a less experienced person have missed?'
3. Document each incident as a case study, not a procedure. Case studies preserve the context and the reasoning. Procedures strip the context away, which is exactly the part that makes expertise valuable.
4. Schedule one interview per week per knowledge holder. Each interview is 45 minutes. After 4 to 6 interviews per person, you have captured the bulk of their non-obvious expertise.
