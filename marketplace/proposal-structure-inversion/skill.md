# Proposal Structure Inversion

Structure the proposal to lead with the status quo cost, then introduce your solution as the path to eliminating that cost.

## Inputs

- `status-quo-cost`: Annual cost of doing nothing
- `cost-breakdown`: Broken down by category

## Outputs

- `inverted-proposal`: The proposal structured as: (1) the cost of the current approach, (2) what your solution changes, (3) the new cost after implementation, (4) the net savings
- `executive-summary`: A 3-sentence executive summary that leads with the status quo cost

## Process

1. Page 1 (executive summary): 'Your current approach to [area] costs approximately $X per year. Our solution reduces that cost to approximately $Y per year. The net improvement is $Z per year, producing an ROI of N:1.'
2. Page 2 (status quo breakdown): show the cost breakdown from the discovery conversation, using the prospect's numbers. This page exists to establish the problem size, not to sell.
3. Page 3 (solution and new state): describe what changes with your product and the resulting cost structure. For each cost category, show the before and after.
4. Page 4 (investment and ROI): your price, the net savings, and the payback period. The price should feel small compared to the status quo cost because the proposal has spent 2 pages establishing how expensive the status quo is.
