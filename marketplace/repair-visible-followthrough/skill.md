# Repair Visible Followthrough

Follow through on the committed changes visibly and consistently over time, because trust is restored only by a pattern of kept commitments that the other person can see, and the repair is not the apology or the promise but the weeks and months of behavior that prove the promise was real.

## Inputs

- `behavioral-change-proposals`: The specific changes proposed
- `change-commitments`: The changes stated as checkable commitments

## Outputs

- `followthrough-plan`: The plan to sustain the committed changes over the time it takes for trust to rebuild, not just in the first days
- `visibility-markers`: How the kept commitments become observable to the other person without you demanding credit for them

## Process

1. Plan to sustain the changes over the long haul, because trust rebuilds on a pattern, not an event. The first week of good behavior proves nothing to someone who was hurt, it is the consistency over months that does.
2. Make the follow-through visible without demanding credit. The changes should be observable in your behavior, but announcing look how reliable I am being turns the repair into a performance and asks them to reward you, which undercuts it.
3. Expect testing and setbacks. The other person may test whether the change is real, and you may slip. A slip handled honestly (naming it, correcting it immediately) can actually strengthen the repair, while a slip hidden confirms the original distrust.
4. Let trust return on their timeline, not yours. Pushing for the relationship to feel normal before they are ready pressures them and signals the follow-through was a means to an end, so keep the behavior steady and let them close the distance when they are ready.
