# Acknowledgment Loop Designer

Design the acknowledgment loop that makes referrers feel valued whether or not the referral converts, sustaining referral behavior over time.

## Inputs

- `referral-attribution-log`: Referrals tracked to their sources
- `super-connector-list`: Top referrers identified

## Outputs

- `acknowledgment-protocol`: The specific actions to take at each stage of a referral (when received, when first contact made, when converted or not converted)
- `referrer-update-cadence`: How often to update referrers on the status of their referrals and the format of those updates

## Process

1. Design a 3-touch acknowledgment protocol. Touch 1 (within 24 hours of the referral): thank the referrer and confirm you will reach out to their contact. This is the most important touch because it validates their effort immediately. Touch 2 (within 1 week): update the referrer that you connected with their contact and share one positive observation ('Sarah was great, we had a productive conversation about X'). Touch 3 (at outcome): whether the referral converted or not, close the loop. If converted: share the positive outcome and credit the referrer. If not converted: thank them and explain briefly without blaming their contact.
2. The key principle: the acknowledgment rewards the ACT of referring, not the OUTCOME. If you only thank people when referrals convert, you are training them to pre-qualify heavily (reducing volume) or to feel bad when referrals do not work out (reducing future attempts). Thank equally for the introduction regardless of what happens next.
3. For super-connectors, add a quarterly recognition. A personal note, a small gift, or a public acknowledgment (with their permission). This is not an incentive payment; it is a signal that you notice and value their contribution to your network.
4. Build the update into your existing cadence system: when a referral-related follow-up is due, include the referrer update as a linked action. Do not track referrals in a separate system from your relationship management. They are part of the same relationship fabric.
