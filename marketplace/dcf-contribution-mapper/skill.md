# Dcf Contribution Mapper

Move the what-happened strand from blame to contribution. Blame asks who is at fault and ends the conversation. Contribution asks how each person added to the situation and keeps it open. Replace certainty about their intent with curiosity about their view.

## Inputs

- `what-happened-conversation`: The facts, stories, and blame from the situation
- `other-party-context`: What you know about the other person's perspective

## Outputs

- `contribution-map`: How each person, including you, contributed to the situation, stated without fault language
- `curiosity-questions`: Open questions that invite the other person's story instead of asserting your own

## Process

1. For each part of the situation, write one line on how you contributed, even if their share is larger. A conversation where only one side contributed is almost always a story, not a fact.
2. Write one line on how they contributed, describing the action and its effect, not the motive you assumed.
3. Convert each assumption about their intent into a question. 'You did not care about the deadline' becomes 'What was going on for you as the deadline approached?'
4. Keep only questions you would genuinely want answered. A question you already know the answer to is a trap, and the other person will feel it.
