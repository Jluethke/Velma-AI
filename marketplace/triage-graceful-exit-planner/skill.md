# Triage Graceful Exit Planner

For the let-go group, plan a graceful exit, because how a relationship ends matters and most relationships deserve a gentle fade or an honest close rather than a cruel cutoff or a cowardly ghosting. The aim is to release the relationship without inflicting unnecessary harm on the other person or on your own peace.

## Inputs

- `disposition-assignments`: The keep, maintain, and let-go assignments
- `boundary-notes`: The boundaries defined for the maintain-with-boundaries group

## Outputs

- `exit-plans`: For each let-go relationship, how it will end: a natural fade, an honest conversation, or a clear boundary, matched to the relationship
- `closure-scripts`: What to say where an explicit close is warranted, said with honesty and without blame

## Process

1. Match the exit to the relationship. A casual relationship can simply fade as you stop initiating, and forcing a dramatic conversation on it would be odd. A close or formal relationship warrants an honest ending rather than a silent fade.
2. Choose honesty over ghosting for relationships that mattered. Disappearing on someone who was genuinely part of your life leaves them with an open loop and leaves you with guilt. A brief honest close serves both of you better.
3. Where an explicit close is warranted, prepare what to say so it lands with care: honest about the relationship changing, free of blame, and without relitigating old grievances. The goal is release, not a verdict on who was wrong.
4. Protect your own peace in the plan. Some relationships cannot be ended cleanly because the other person will not allow it, and in those cases a firm boundary (or, in genuinely harmful cases, a clean break) is the graceful option available to you.
