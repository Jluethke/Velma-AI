# Feedback Source Selector

Choose 3 people who observed the behavior and who have different perspectives on it.

## Inputs

- `feedback-targets`: The specific things you want feedback on
- `recent-performance`: The context of the performance

## Outputs

- `feedback-sources`: 3 people to ask, each chosen for a different perspective (e.g., a peer, a direct report, a client or audience member)
- `source-rationale`: Why each person was chosen and what perspective they bring

## Process

1. Choose people who were actually present and paid attention. Second-hand feedback ('I heard it went well') is useless.
2. Choose from different vantage points. A peer, a subordinate, and a customer see different things. A peer notices technical quality. A subordinate notices communication clarity. A customer notices relevance.
3. Include at least one person who will be honest even if the feedback is negative. This is usually someone with high trust and no political reason to flatter you.
4. Avoid asking only people who will agree with your self-assessment. The value of feedback is in the delta between your perception and theirs.
