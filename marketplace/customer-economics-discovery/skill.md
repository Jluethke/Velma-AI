# Customer Economics Discovery

Understand the customer's economics: how they make money, what their costs are, and where your product fits in their value chain.

## Inputs

- `customer-profile`: Who the customer is and what their business or life looks like
- `product-description`: What your product or service does for them

## Outputs

- `customer-economics`: How the customer generates revenue, their cost structure, and where your product saves them money or helps them earn more
- `value-mechanism`: The specific mechanism by which your product creates value (saves time, reduces errors, increases revenue, reduces risk, improves quality)

## Process

1. Interview 5-10 customers (or prospective customers) about their economics. Ask: how do you make money? What are your biggest costs? What would happen if [the problem your product solves] got 50% worse? What would happen if it got 50% better?
2. Identify the specific value mechanism. Your product does one or more of: saves time (which has a dollar value), reduces errors (which have a cost), increases revenue (which is measurable), reduces risk (which has an expected cost), or improves quality (which affects pricing or retention).
3. Quantify the value mechanism in the customer's terms, not yours. 'Our software saves 10 hours per week' means nothing. 'Our software saves 10 hours per week of a $75/hour employee, which is $39,000 per year' means something.
4. Verify the quantification with the customer. Ask: 'Does $39,000 per year in savings sound about right based on your experience?' If they say it is higher, you have more pricing room. If lower, adjust.
