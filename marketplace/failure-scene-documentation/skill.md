# Failure Scene Documentation

Document the failed component in its as-received condition before any cleaning or sectioning, preserving evidence of the failure mechanism.

## Inputs

- `failed-component`: The failed part with any fragments, including service history documentation (operating hours, load history, maintenance records)
- `failure-description`: Description of the failure event (sudden vs progressive, operating conditions at time of failure, any abnormal conditions preceding failure)

## Outputs

- `documented-evidence`: Photographic record and dimensional measurements of the failed component in as-received condition, with fracture origin area identified
- `initial-hypothesis`: Preliminary failure mechanism hypothesis based on macroscopic examination (overload, fatigue, corrosion, creep, or combination)

## Process

1. Photograph the complete component from multiple angles before touching it. Include a scale reference in every image. Document the location and orientation of all fracture surfaces, cracks, wear patterns, corrosion products, and deformation. Sketch the component with dimensions and mark the fracture location relative to geometric features (holes, fillets, welds, surface transitions).
2. Examine the fracture surface macroscopically. Beach marks (concentric lines emanating from the origin) indicate fatigue. A flat, granular surface with no deformation indicates brittle fracture. A fibrous surface with necking indicates ductile overload. Ratchet marks at the surface indicate multiple fatigue initiation sites. Intergranular facets visible to the naked eye suggest stress corrosion cracking, creep, or hydrogen embrittlement.
3. Identify the fracture origin. It is the point from which all fracture features (beach marks, chevron marks, river lines) radiate. The origin is almost always at a stress concentration: a machining mark, a corrosion pit, an inclusion, a weld toe, or a design feature (sharp corner, keyway). Preserving the origin area undamaged is the single most critical step in the investigation.
4. Protect the fracture surfaces immediately. Do not mate the fracture halves together (this damages the microscopic evidence). Do not clean with solvents yet (corrosion products on the fracture surface are evidence). Apply a thin coat of light oil if the surfaces must be stored before SEM examination. Bag and label each piece separately.
